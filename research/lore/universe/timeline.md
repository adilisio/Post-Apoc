# Fallout Universe — Master Timeline

Scope: point of divergence to the latest dated event in any game, plus the Amazon TV series (flagged `[TV]`).
Purpose: reference for how a long-running setting stacks its history, so we can design our own.

Tag key: `[DISPUTED]` sources conflict · `[RETCON]` a later work changed an earlier one · `[SEMI-CANON]` from
the Fallout Bible, cancelled projects, or non-mainline games · `[UNVERIFIED]` recalled from general knowledge
of the games and **not** re-checked against a source in this research session (Nukapedia blocked automated
access; see Gaps) · `[TV]` established by the Amazon series.

## Quick Reference

- **There is no single agreed point of divergence.** The original team's concept was "the future as the
  1950s imagined it", not a forked-history date. The Fallout Bible says the split came after WWII; later
  games add earlier oddities (a 1969 reorganisation into 13 commonwealths; a "Virgo II" Moon landing in 1969).
- The "transistor was never invented / never miniaturised" explanation is a **popular fan gloss** `[DISPUTED]`,
  useful as a rule of thumb (tubes and fusion instead of microchips) but not a hard canon fact.
- **Resource Wars: April 2052 – 23 October 2077.** Oil runs out; Europe and the Middle East fight until the
  fields are dry (2052–2060); the UN dissolves (July 2052).
- **Sino-American War: winter 2066 – 2077.** China invades Alaska for its oil; the US annexes Canada
  (begun 2072, complete January 2076) and retakes Anchorage (10/11 January 2077).
- **Great War: Saturday 23 October 2077**, roughly two hours. First launches detected 09:13 EST, impacts in
  Pennsylvania and New York 09:42, monitoring stations dark by 09:47.
- **Who fired first is deliberately unknown.** In-game voices disagree; Tim Cain has said both that the
  original team's private assumption was China (provoked by US bioweapon work) and that this is not canon.
  The TV series adds Vault-Tec and an Enclave figure as suspects without resolving it.
- **Game order by in-universe date:** 76 (2102–2105+), Fallout 1 (2161–62), Tactics (2197, semi-canon),
  Fallout 2 (2241–42), Fallout 3 (2277), New Vegas (2281), Fallout 4 (2287), TV series (2296).
- Release order is very different from chronological order: the newest-released game (76) is the earliest
  set, which is why it generated the most retcon disputes.
- The setting spans **219 years after the bombs** — by 2296 it is "post-post-apocalyptic", and several
  writers (Avellone, the TV showrunners) have chosen to knock civilisation back down.
- **NCR**: Shady Sands founded 2142; republic formed 2186–2189; capital destroyed by a nuclear device some
  time after 2281 `[TV]`.
- **Brotherhood of Steel**: founded from US Army deserters at Mariposa, October–November 2077; eastern
  expedition reaches Washington DC c. 2254–2255.
- **Caesar's Legion**: founded 2247 in Arizona; loses the First Battle of Hoover Dam 2277.
- **Enclave**: pre-war shadow government; oil rig destroyed 2242; Raven Rock destroyed 2277; reappears in `[TV]` 2296.
- The Fallout Bible (2002) is the backbone for almost every pre-2242 date, yet its own author says it is
  not canon. Treat pre-war month-level dates as "Bible dates adopted by later games".

## 1. The divergence and the premise

| Source | What it says | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Leonard Boyarsky / Tim Cain (original team, 1995–97) | The setting is the future as imagined by 1950s science fiction, adopted in late 1995 as a filter for a previously loose pile of ideas. *Forbidden Planet* (1956) was shown to artists as the tech reference. | Developer intent (via Cain's channel summaries and secondary reporting) |
| Fallout Bible (Avellone, 2002) | History follows ours until after WWII, then diverges. | `[SEMI-CANON]` |
| Fallout 3 / 4 environmental lore | US Space Administration; Capt. Carl Bell's Defiance 7 flight ends in a fatal crash 5 May 1961; Virgo II lands on the Moon 1969; 13 commonwealths formed 1969. | Canon (in-game) |
| Fan consensus | The transistor was never invented/miniaturised, so computing stayed bulky while nuclear tech raced ahead. | `[DISPUTED]` — transistors and microprocessors are occasionally mentioned in-game `[UNVERIFIED]`; no developer has fixed this as the rule |

Design-relevant reading: the premise is an **aesthetic rule**, not an alternate-history date. Everything that
followed (nuclear cars, household robots, monochrome terminals, no social change since ~1958) is derived from
"what would the 1950s have extrapolated?".

### Who fired first — what each source says

| Source | Year | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| Fallout 1 intro / manual | 1997 | Nobody knows; it no longer matters. Sets the franchise position. |
| Fallout 2 — President Richardson | 1998 | "those damn Reds launched everything they had" — an Enclave partisan, unreliable by design |
| Fallout Bible | 2002 | Explicitly "unknown", China or America `[SEMI-CANON]` |
| Fallout 3 — survivor testimony | 2008 | DC got warning after the West Coast was hit; no blame assigned |
| Fallout 4 — Switchboard log | 2015 | US detects launches then orders a *response* scenario; implies the US was reacting, but never names the launcher |
| Fallout 4 — Captain Zao | 2015 | Chinese submarine captain launched "as ordered"; does not say it was the first strike |
| Tim Cain — TKs-Mantis interview | Oct 2023 | Team's assumption: China launched, because the US kept researching FEV after promising to stop ("all we did is move it"). Reported as saying this is not canon and that Bethesda defines canon |
| Tim Cain — other statements | 2023–24 | Also quoted saying he doesn't know and "It was probably some rogue nation" `[DISPUTED]` — his statements are not fully consistent across reports |
| `[TV]` Season 1 | 2024 | In a 2077 flashback a Vault-Tec executive proposes guaranteeing the war by dropping the bombs themselves |
| `[TV]` Season 2 | 2025–26 | Still unresolved: House believes an unknown third party; an Enclave-linked scientist is said to have proposed striking first |

Conclusion: the ambiguity is a **protected feature**. Every new work adds a suspect rather than an answer.

## Gaps

- Nukapedia (fallout.fandom.com) returned HTTP 402 to automated fetches; fallout.wiki pages were read only in
  100k-character windows. Many day-level dates for Fallout 3/NV/4 are therefore tagged `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- Tim Cain's primary videos were not viewed; his position is reconstructed from press reports that disagree.
- In-universe years for Fallout 76 content after Atlantic City (2105) were not confirmed.
- The exact year of the Shady Sands bombing rests on one secondary source.

## Design Takeaways

- **Fix an aesthetic rule, not a divergence date.** "The future the 1950s expected" generated thirty years of
  content; a hard fork date would only have generated arguments. Our setting needs an equivalent one-line filter.
- **Keep the cataclysm's cause unknowable, and say so on page one.** Fallout's refusal to say who fired first
  keeps the theme universal ("war never changes"). Decide which of our mysteries are permanently closed boxes
  and write that into the bible so no later writer "solves" them.
- **Unreliable narrators are the tool** for that: every in-world account of the war is partisan, so nothing
  contradicts anything.
- **One dense day beats a vague war.** The minute-by-minute 23 October log is the most memorable piece of
  timeline in the franchise. Give our catastrophe a date, a weekday, and a clock time.
- **Leave big blank decades.** The best-loved additions (NCR, Legion) grew in gaps the first game left empty.
  Fallout 76 shows the cost of filling a gap *before* existing events: it must contradict or tiptoe.
- **Prequels are retcon machines.** If we ever go backwards in time, pick a region nobody has described.
- **Moving the clock forward creates the "post-post-apocalypse" problem.** By 2281 there is a nation-state
  with taxes. Decide early whether our world is allowed to recover, and how far, rather than nuking the
  recovering capital off-screen later.
- **Never canonise a player-choice ending unless forced.** New Vegas's refusal, and the show's 15-year-later
  "fog", preserve every player's version. Design endings so a sequel can be vague about them.
- **A chalkboard can start a canon war.** Any date shown on screen is a commitment. Keep one authoritative,
  versioned timeline file and check every prop, terminal and loading screen against it.
- **Date pre-catastrophe events to the month, post-catastrophe events to the year.** That is what Fallout
  effectively does, and it matches how records would survive.
