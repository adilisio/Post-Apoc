# Fallout 76 (2018-2026) — Lore and Canon Reference

Scope: the complete story and world canon of Fallout 76 (Bethesda Game Studios, released 14 November 2018) from
launch through the most recent update found at time of writing (The Slasher, 15 September 2026). This file
covers lore only; systems (C.A.M.P., perks, seasons, monetisation) are mentioned only where they shape the fiction.

Confidence tags used: `[RETCON]`, `[DISPUTED]`, `[SEMI-CANON]`, `[UNVERIFIED]` (see CONVENTIONS.md). Items
tagged `[UNVERIFIED]` come from the researcher's recollection and could not be re-checked against a source in
this pass.

## Quick Reference

- **Earliest game in the timeline.** Opens on "Reclamation Day", 23 October 2102, exactly 25 years after the
  Great War (23 Oct 2077) and 59 years before Fallout 1 (2161). The live game has since advanced to about 2105.
- **Setting:** "Appalachia" = post-war West Virginia, later extended into Virginia's Shenandoah (Skyline Valley,
  2024) and south-east Ohio (Burning Springs, 2025), with instanced trips to Pittsburgh and Atlantic City.
- **Protagonist:** one of the residents of Vault 76, a *control* vault holding 500 of "America's best and
  brightest", tasked with rebuilding. No fixed name, backstory or voice; every player is one of the dwellers.
- **Launch premise:** every human in the region is already dead or gone. The story is archaeology: you follow
  the Overseer's holotapes and reconstruct the fall of five factions from terminals, notes and corpses.
- **The threat:** the Scorched Plague, spread by scorchbeasts (giant mutated bats) that the local Enclave bred
  and released; it turns people into hive-minded, crystal-crusted "Scorched". Each faction held a piece of the
  solution and none would cooperate, so all died.
- **The launch ending:** inoculate yourself, join every dead faction posthumously, take over the Enclave's
  bunker, launch a nuclear missile at the main scorchbeast nest and kill the Scorchbeast Queen.
- **Wastelanders (April 2020, set 2103)** reversed the "no human NPCs" rule: people return, with two hub
  settlements (Foundation and Crater), dialogue trees with skill checks, reputation, allies and a gold heist
  on Vault 79.
- **Brotherhood arc (2020-21):** a small expedition from the Californian Brotherhood arrives; the story is an
  ideological split between its two leaders (help people vs. follow the code), resolved by the player.
- **Later story drops** are smaller: Pittsburgh and Atlantic City "Expeditions", a storm-wracked valley with a
  vault of electrically-charged ghouls, becoming a ghoul yourself, an Ohio warlord, and a 2026 serial-killer cult.
- **Cryptids are the game's most praised lore decision:** Mothman, the Flatwoods Monster, the Grafton Monster,
  the Snallygaster, Sheepsquatch and others come straight from real regional folklore.
- **Biggest lore controversies:** a Brotherhood of Steel chapter in West Virginia in the 2080s, super mutants
  on the east coast in 2102, an Enclave cell, and the general feeling of series icons being placed everywhere.
  Bethesda's answer in almost every case: an isolated local origin that destroys itself before later games.
- **Critical arc:** Metacritic about 52 (PC) at launch, 68 for Wastelanders (PC); over 17 million players
  reported by March 2024 and a record day of over a million logins after the TV series in April 2024.
- **Tone:** lighter and stranger than the single-player games — folk horror, roadside Americana, bluegrass and
  autumn forest instead of grey rubble.

### 1.2 Place

Appalachia is a fictionalised, compressed West Virginia built from real landmarks: the State Capitol in
Charleston, the New River Gorge Bridge, the Greenbrier resort (as "the Whitespring"), Camden Park, West
Virginia University (as "Vault-Tec University" in Morgantown), Point Pleasant and its Mothman museum,
Helvetia, Harpers Ferry, Spruce Knob, Seneca Rocks, the Green Bank radio observatory (as the "National
Isolated Radio Array") and the Trans-Allegheny Lunatic Asylum (as "Allegheny Asylum" / Fort Defiance).
Because the state took few direct hits, the world is green: standing forests, autumn colour, intact small
towns. The damage is mostly *industrial* and *mutational* rather than blast damage.

The state's Tourism Office formally partnered with Bethesda in October 2018 to promote real-world visits
(PC Gamer, GameSpot, WV Public Broadcasting). Hard visitor figures were not found in this pass `[UNVERIFIED]`.

### 1.3 Pre-war Appalachia (the backstory everything hangs on)

- **Automation vs. labour.** Two mining dynasties, Hornwright Industrial and Garrahan Mining, competed to
  automate coal mining (Hornwright's auto-miners vs. Garrahan's Excavator power armour). Miners lost their jobs
  to robots; riots followed. The Ash Heap is the scar.
- **Atomic Mining Services (AMS)** discovered *ultracite*, a glowing green super-ore, and built Watoga, a fully
  automated "city of the future", in the Cranberry Bog.
- **West Tek** ran a research centre near Huntersville and secretly dosed the town's water with the Forced
  Evolutionary Virus (FEV) from 2076, under an Army quarantine.
- **Vault-Tec** built Vaults 51, 63, 76, 79, 94 and 96 in the region and ran a university to train overseers.
- **The federal government** hid a continuity-of-government bunker under the Whitespring resort, three
  automated ICBM silos (Sites Alpha, Bravo, Charlie), and the nation's gold reserve (moved to Vault 79).
- **The Free States** were pre-war secessionist survivalists who built private bunkers in the swampy north-east.
- **Cold-War paranoia** is everywhere: Chinese "Liberator" propaganda robots dropped on the region, a
  signals-intelligence base at Sugar Grove, anti-communist drills in schools.

## 2. Protagonist and Premise

- **Vault 76** is one of Vault-Tec's rare control vaults: no cruel experiment, just 500 high achievers and a
  mandate to recolonise. `[RETCON]` Terminal text in Fallout 3 implied it would open 20 years after a war;
  Fallout 76 opens it at 25 years.
- **Reclamation Day.** The player oversleeps after the farewell party, walks out through an empty vault being
  shut down by cheerful Mister Handy robots, and collects a C.A.M.P. (portable building device) on the way.
- **The Overseer** is an unnamed West Virginia native and Vault-Tec University graduate. She left before
  everyone else because Vault-Tec gave her a secret second mission: secure the three nuclear silos. She leaves
  a trail of holotapes at camps and at places from her own life (her home, her old school, her fiancé's
  workplace). The tapes are the emotional spine of the launch game: she discovers the region is dead, finds her
  fiancé turned Scorched and kills him `[UNVERIFIED detail]`, and gradually concludes the silos must be *used*.
- **The player's role** is literally "the people who came after": an investigator and inheritor. Every launch
  quest giver is a robot, an AI, a recording or a corpse.
- **Shared identity.** All players are residents of 76, which neatly justifies dozens of jumpsuited strangers
  running around; the wikis refer to them collectively as the Vault Dwellers or Residents.

## 3. Launch-Era Main Plot (2018) — Told Through Holotapes and Terminals

Bethesda's stated design at launch was that every human you meet is another player. The consequence: the
main quest is a chain of dead ends where you always arrive too late.

| Step | Quest(s) | Region | What you learn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reclamation Day, First Contact | Forest | Flatwoods was a Responders aid post; everyone is dead. You enrol as a volunteer via terminals |
| 2 | Thirst Things First, Second Helpings | Forest | Survival tutorials disguised as Responder volunteer training (boil water, cook, test samples) |
| 3 | Final Departure, An Ounce of Prevention | Forest | Morgantown Airport, the Responders' last HQ, was overrun. A medical AI walks you through finishing a Scorched vaccine and inoculating yourself |
| 4 | Into the Fire | Forest / Ash Heap | You pass the exam for the Fire Breathers, the Responders' elite who fought in burning mines |
| 5 | The Missing Link, Signal Strength | Savage Divide | Trail of a missing Responder agent leads to the raiders' mountain stronghold, Top of the World |
| 6 | Flavors of Mayhem, Key to the Past | Savage Divide | Rose, a reprogrammed Miss Nanny robot with a dead raider's personality, makes you prove yourself and recover the five raider gangs' key fragments |
| 7 | Early Warnings, Reassembly Required, Coming to Fruition | Mire | The Free States built a Scorched Detection System; a dead engineer's recordings guide you to finish it |
| 8 | Recruitment Blues, Back to Basic | Forest / Mire | To access military systems you must enlist: a robot-run boot camp and a deliberately Kafkaesque DMV queue for ID |
| 9 | Defiance Has Fallen, Belly of the Beast | Cranberry Bog | The Brotherhood's last stand at Fort Defiance; you descend into the Glassed Cavern where their commander died |
| 10 | Uncle Sam, Bunker Buster | Mire | The trail of ex-senator Sam Blackwell, a Free States founder, leads to a hidden bunker |
| 11 | One of Us, Officer on Deck | Savage Divide | The bunker's AI, MODUS, recruits you into the Enclave; you grind commendations to become a "general" |
| 12 | I Am Become Death, Mission: Countdown | Savage Divide | Collect code fragments, decrypt them, fight through a silo and launch a nuke |
| 13 | Scorched Earth (public event) | Cranberry Bog | Nuking Fissure Site Prime draws out the Scorchbeast Queen; killing her is the de facto ending |

**The moral of the launch story:** each faction had one piece. The Responders had the vaccine and the
manpower, the Free States had the detection system, the Brotherhood had the firepower and knew where the nest
was, the Raiders had numbers, the Enclave had the nukes. Pride, paranoia and old grudges kept them apart.
The vault dwellers succeed only because they are willing to be all five at once.

**The irony critics noticed:** the solution to a post-nuclear world is more nuclear weapons, launched
repeatedly by players as a loot-farming activity. The game itself never interrogates this.

### 3.1 The Scorched Plague and the scorchbeasts

- Origin (fallout.wiki): Enclave scientists experimenting on irradiated bats produced the first scorchbeast.
  Enclave leader Thomas Eckhart had them studied in an old AMS mine (later the Glassed Cavern) and released
  them in 2086 so that the automated defence network would register an invasion, raise the alert to DEFCON 1
  and unlock the missile silos for him. `[DISPUTED]` Some in-game text and fan readings attribute the bats'
  mutation partly to ultracite mining; the wiki's summary treats the Enclave experiments as the origin.
- Scorchbeasts carry a pathogen that crusts victims in ultracite-like crystal and links them to a hive mind.
  The Scorched keep enough motor memory to use guns, which is the fiction that lets a game with no human NPCs
  still have gun-wielding humanoid enemies.
- Scorchbeasts tunnel under the region and emerge at "fissure sites"; Fissure Site Prime in the Cranberry Bog
  is the queen's nest. The stated stakes are continental: if the plague leaves Appalachia, humanity ends.
- Wastelanders adds a mass vaccination: the Overseer and the player distribute the vaccine to the returning
  population by mixing it into a soft-drink production line `[UNVERIFIED detail]`.

Notes:

- **The Mire's overgrowth** is explained by Vault 94: a pacifist, faith-based commune that opened one year
  after the war with a G.E.C.K. (terraforming kit). Outsiders, suspicious of its generosity, massacred it; the
  damaged G.E.C.K. ran wild and produced the "strangler" flora. Vault 94 was a three-part raid from August
  2019 until its removal in 2020.
- **Watoga's robots** turned on the population at the moment of the war, so the "city of the future" never had
  a post-war human history. `[UNVERIFIED]` The sabotage is attributed in terminals to an insider grudge.
- **Mole miners** are short, masked, mining-suited humanoids; the game deliberately leaves their origin vague
  (trapped miners who mutated underground). Later updates gave them a merchant, the Purveyor.
- **Instanced, off-map locations:** the Pitt (Pittsburgh, 2022) and Atlantic City (2023-24), reached by
  vertibird from the Whitespring Refuge; the Gleaming Depths raid (2024) under the Ash Heap.

## 6. Wastelanders (14 April 2020): Bringing People Back

**Why it happened.** The absence of human NPCs was the single most criticised launch decision (Metacritic about
52 on PC). Wastelanders was a free relaunch-scale update, delayed from late 2019, and was received as the
point where the game became a recognisable Fallout RPG (PC Metacritic 68; IGN noted it "finally introduces
mostly interesting human NPCs").

**Fictional justification.** It is now 2103. Word has spread that Appalachia hides a great treasure, and that
the vault dwellers have blunted the Scorched threat, so two waves of outsiders arrive.

**Plot outline:**

1. **The Wayward.** A bar outside Vault 76 run by Duchess. A tutorial arc about a local gang and a dead
   treasure hunter named Crane, whose "treasure" is a decoy but confirms that something real exists.
2. **The Overseer returns** in person at her family home. She insists the newcomers must be vaccinated before
   the plague takes them, which requires winning over both settlements.
3. **Foundation and Crater.** You run errands for both, building reputation (seven tiers from Hostile to Ally).
4. **Vault 79.** The treasure is the US gold reserve, relocated from Fort Knox before the war. You choose
   which faction's specialist crew to take on the break-in — the point of no return — then fight through the
   vault's defences and its ghoulified former guards, and meet the surviving Secret Service agents.
5. **The split.** At the end you decide who gets the gold: Settlers, Raiders, both, or yourself. The Overseer
   hopes it can back a new currency and restart an economy. The choice affects reputation and dialogue, not
   the shared world.

**New storytelling tools:** full dialogue trees with S.P.E.C.I.A.L. and perk checks (a deliberate return to
Fallout 3 / New Vegas style rather than Fallout 4's wheel), instanced interiors so each player sees their own
world state, daily faction quests, and **allies** who live at your camp.

| Ally | Hook | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Beckett | Ex-Blood Eagle trying to rescue his brother from the gang | Full questline, romanceable, sets up a bar at your camp |
| Commander Sofia Daguerre | Pre-war astronaut who woke from cryosleep and crash-landed | Questline uncovers a pre-war conspiracy behind her mission; romanceable |
| Lite allies (settler and raider wanderers, etc.) | No story | Give repeatable errands only |
| Later additions (2020-2025) | A chef, a doctor, Brotherhood-adjacent mercenaries, an alien, a ghoul physicist, an Atlantic City fixer and others | Mostly unlocked through seasons; minor stories `[UNVERIFIED full list]` |

**The cost to the launch tone.** Wastelanders softens "everyone died" into "many left and came back", and
turns the lonely archaeology of 2018 into an optional prologue. New players now meet friendly humans within
minutes of leaving the vault.

## 7. The Brotherhood First Expeditionary Force (Steel Dawn, Nov 2020; Steel Reign, 7 July 2021)

**Setup (fallout.wiki).** The Californian Brotherhood lost contact with Taggerdy's chapter and never learned
its fate. Paladin Leila Rahmani, a former National Guard officer, persuades Elder Maxson and the council to
send an expedition: inspect technology sites en route, occupy the ATLAS Observatory, find out what happened,
report back. Five set out; three arrive.

**The original sin.** Crossing the country, Knight Shin's detachment armed a settlement with military weapons
to help it resist raiders. The raiders won, took the weapons, and killed almost everyone; two children
survived and travel with the expedition. Rahmani and Shin disagree about whether this must be confessed to the
elders, and about what the Brotherhood is *for*.

| | Paladin Rahmani | Knight Shin |
|---|---|---|
| Priority | Protect people now; adapt the mission to local reality | The code and chain of command; technology control |
| Defining act | Destroys the transmitter that could reconnect them to Lost Hills, fearing recall | Declares she is no longer Brotherhood |
| Reading | Humanitarian, but lies to her own people | Honourable, but rigid and willing to execute prisoners |

**Steel Dawn** establishes Fort Atlas, recruits locals, involves the player in recovering stolen weapons and
dealing with Foundation and Crater's interest in them, and ends with a super mutant assault on the fort.

**Steel Reign** traces the assault to Dr. Edgar Blackburn, a scientist whom Shin had turned away. He has been
kidnapping people and refining FEV, using Vault 96 and the West Tek facility. He exposes himself to his own
strain, becomes a behemoth and is killed. The player then decides the fate of his surviving colleagues and
finally sides with Rahmani or Shin; the loser is killed or sent back west, and the winner commands the chapter.

**Why it works:** it uses the Brotherhood to ask the question the series keeps circling — hoarding technology
vs. helping people — at the moment the organisation's doctrine is still being written, and lets the player
set the local answer. **Why it is safe canon:** the chapter is cut off from California by its own leader, so
nothing here needs to be known by later games.

Release-date note: Steel Dawn was scheduled for 1 December 2020 and went live about a week early after an
accidental console release; sources list 21 or 24 November `[DISPUTED exact date]`.

### 8.2 Expeditions

- **The Pitt (2022).** Pittsburgh a full 175 years before Fallout 3's visit. The Union, mechanically gifted
  workers, are trying to retake the city from the Fanatics; the toxic environment is already degrading
  residents into Trogs. Led in development by Jeff Gardiner, who also oversaw the Fallout 3 add-on. Lore
  value: shows a famous later location at an earlier stage without contradicting it.
- **Atlantic City (2023-24).** A storm-battered, half-flooded resort city run by three rival institutions.
  Missions include rigging a lethal game show for the Showmen and dealing with the Family's criminal business;
  the second half adds the Overgrown and a questline about a mob family that relocated to Appalachia and needs
  a cure for a relative's addiction to a drug derived from the Jersey Devil.

Both are repeatable four-player instances with thin but characterful stories, launched by vertibird.

### 8.3 Skyline Valley (2024)

- **Region:** Shenandoah, south of the original map; a red lightning storm circles permanently overhead.
- **Vault 63:** its overseer, Hugo Stolz (blind from birth, a Vault-Tec board member and industrialist), ran a
  weather-control project from the Hawksbill Weather Station. The machine's activations ghoulified the vault
  and turned many dwellers into the Lost: mindless, electrically charged ghouls. Hugo himself was altered and
  can influence the Lost.
- **Conflict:** Hugo believes controlling the Lost is the only way to save them and wants to fire the machine
  again; his daughter Audrey opposes him; Chief Oberlin, who now runs the vault, wants Hugo captured alive.
- **Ending (three options):** kill Hugo (Audrey then shoots Oberlin and takes charge), subdue him and hand him
  to Oberlin (Audrey feels betrayed), or let him activate the machine and remain in control.
- **Events:** a storm-chasing public event, and a nuke-triggered boss fight against robots.
- Reception: welcomed as the first new land mass in six years; story judged short.

### 8.4 Ghoul Within (2025): the playable ghoul

- Quest "Leap of Faith" (available from level 50): a distress call leads to a dying man with radiation
  sickness. A ghoul specialist, Parthenia Blankenship, offers to save him by turning him into a ghoul, which
  needs irradiated ore from a disposal site. The player influences a confrontation with a scavenger and then
  advises the patient: become a ghoul and join the Revenants, or die human among friends.
- The follow-up quest lets the player undergo the same procedure. It is reversible.
- **Mechanics as fiction:** hunger and thirst are replaced by a *Feral* meter; radiation heals and grants
  *Glow* (bonus survivability); a separate set of ghoul perks; some human factions turn hostile, so a disguise
  system exists to keep quests accessible `[UNVERIFIED disguise details]`.
- `[DISPUTED]` Deliberate, on-demand ghoulification sits awkwardly with older lore in which becoming a ghoul
  rather than dying was unpredictable. The update follows the TV series' popular ghoul lead.

### 8.5 Burning Springs (2025)

- **Region:** south-east Ohio as an arid, rust-coloured frontier — chosen, per the creative director, because
  Ohio was a blank slate in series lore. Billed as the largest update since Wastelanders, with a deliberately
  New Vegas-like dusty tone (GamesRadar, PCGamesN).
- **The Rust King:** an intelligent super mutant who was an Enclave prisoner aboard a vertibird; he killed the
  pilot, survived the crash, and built a kingdom on the principle that the strong rule. He lost an eye fighting
  a deathclaw matriarch and raised her eggs into the first tamed deathclaws. He conquered Fort Steuben,
  reached terms with the Ash Cavers in 2089, and fortified the border against West Virginia's Mothman cult.
- **Main quest (four parts):** Welcoming Committee, Might Makes Right, Insurrection, When the Rust Settles.
  The player aids an apparent resistance, is betrayed by a contact who was using them as bait, survives the
  arena, and is declared the Rust King's champion regardless of earlier choices. A follow-up, "An Audience
  with the King" (11 Dec 2025), continues it. Notably *less* branching than Wastelanders.
- **The Ghoul:** Walton Goggins voices his TV character, placed in Highway Town as the broker for a new bounty
  hunting system (solo "grunt hunts" and public "head hunts"). Because the character lives for two more
  centuries, the cameo is timeline-compatible; whether it is binding for the show is left open by developers.

### 8.6 2026

- **The Backwoods (March):** primarily a systems overhaul. Bigfoot arrives as one of several bosses with a 33%
  chance to crash the end of certain public events. Earlier lore had seeded Bigfoot through pre-war magazines,
  a radio serial and footprints added in Burning Springs; some wastelanders worship "squatches".
- **Infestations (June):** no narrative of note.
- **The Slasher (September):** a seasonal, week-by-week story. A cult called the Pint-Sized Phantoms venerates
  a pre-war child serial killer (a figure first referenced in Fallout 3's Tranquility Lane) and tries to
  resurrect him as a spirit inhabiting empty power armour. Four quests (Masked Truth, Secrets to the Grave,
  Out of the Shadows, Blood Will Have Blood), delivered via radio news broadcasts. The quest-by-quest
  resolution was not documented in sources at time of writing `[UNVERIFIED]`.

### 9.2 Why the regional-folklore approach was praised

- **It gave the map an identity no other Fallout has.** Reviewers who disliked the launch game still singled
  out the creatures; GamesRadar interviewed a West Virginia historian about the real legends, and local
  coverage treated the game as a showcase for state folklore.
- **Specificity reads as respect.** Using the actual towns attached to the legends (Flatwoods, Grafton, Point
  Pleasant) made exploration double as a folklore tour.
- **It solves an enemy-variety problem honestly.** A setting only 25 years post-war cannot plausibly have
  centuries of evolved mutants; pre-existing legends (and pre-war labs) fill the bestiary without it.
- **Ambiguity is preserved.** The game rarely explains them fully. Mothman may just watch you from a ridge and
  vanish. Pre-war "truth seeker" notes debunk some sightings and confirm others.
- **It is extensible.** Each new location brings its own legend (Jersey Devil, Ohio cryptids), giving updates
  an easy hook; a Bethesda developer has said that if a place has a native cryptid, it must appear.
- **It supports multiple tones:** horror (Wendigo), comedy (the Sheepsquatch impostor), awe (Wise Mothman).

## 12. Themes and Tone

- **Cooperation or extinction.** The launch thesis; restated in Wastelanders (two factions, one vaccine) and
  Steel Reign (two leaders, one chapter).
- **Institutions outliving people.** Robots, AIs and procedures keep running. Much of the humour and most of
  the melancholy come from this.
- **Corporate and state betrayal of rural communities.** Mining automation, poisoned water, a town used as a
  lab: grounded in real Appalachian history of extraction.
- **Reclamation rather than survival.** The player is a rebuilder by mandate; the camp-building system is the
  mechanical expression of the theme.
- **Folk weirdness over grimdark.** Monsters in the fog, parades, moonshine, banjo and country radio.

## Design Takeaways

- **Pick a real region and commit to its specifics.** Local legends, industries, festivals and grievances gave
  this map more character than any amount of generic ruin. For our game: choose a place, research its folklore
  and labour history, and let those generate enemies, factions and quests.
- **Folklore creatures are a gift: pre-loved, ambiguous, extensible.** Keep some unexplained. Let at least one
  be harmless or helpful, one be a hoax, and one be worse than the stories.
- **Environmental storytelling is a spice, not a meal.** A world of only recordings made every quest end at a
  corpse and removed consequence. Use the archaeology layer for *history*, and living characters for *stakes*.
- **The "five factions each held a piece" structure is excellent** and reusable: a tragedy the player
  reconstructs and then corrects. It would land harder if some of those people were still alive to persuade.
- **Do not kill your world before the player arrives, then walk it back.** Decide at the start whether the
  setting is empty or inhabited; the Wastelanders reversal cost the launch story its meaning.
- **Avoid importing series mascots where they do not belong.** Each "isolated local origin that wipes itself
  out" was defensible alone and corrosive together. In an original setting, let each region have factions that
  could only exist there.
- **Small expedition, big argument.** The Rahmani/Shin split shows how to stage ideology cheaply: two credible
  leaders, one shared guilt, and a player who must pick. Worth copying in structure.
- **Let technology and society visibly change over time.** A world where currency, weapons and monsters are
  identical across two centuries feels static. Give our timeline eras with distinct tech and customs.
- **Plan the live-service clock.** If the game runs for years, decide how in-world time advances and what is
  allowed to change permanently. Otherwise stories become weightless ("champion regardless of choices").
- **Shared-world choices need a home.** Instanced interiors and personal reputation let choices exist without
  breaking a shared map; this is a workable pattern if we ever do co-op.
- **Robots carrying on rituals for no one** is one of the most effective, cheapest tone devices available:
  funny at first sight, sad at second.
- **Avoid making the apocalypse's cause the endgame toy.** Player-launched nukes as loot farming undercut the
  setting's central warning. If we include a catastrophic power, using it should cost something.
- **Guest characters and crossovers date quickly.** The TV cameo is fun marketing but adds nothing to the
  world. Keep our canon self-sufficient.
- **Give the player-character a communal identity.** "One of the people from the vault" is a simple, elegant
  frame for both solo and multiplayer play, with a mandate (rebuild) that explains the core loop.
