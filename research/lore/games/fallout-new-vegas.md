# Fallout: New Vegas (2010) — Lore and Canon Reference

Scope: the base game (Obsidian Entertainment, published by Bethesda Softworks, released 19 October 2010) and
its four story add-ons: Dead Money, Honest Hearts, Old World Blues, Lonesome Road. This file describes and
analyses; it does not reproduce game text. Quest titles are given because they are the handles other sources
use.

Confidence note: the ending-slide tables, release dates, timeline dates and developer statements were checked
against sources this session (see `## Sources`). Much of the fine plot detail (who says what, which skill
check opens which route) is written from general knowledge of the game and was only spot-checked; anything I
was actively unsure of carries a tag. Treat exact skill thresholds as `[UNVERIFIED]` unless stated otherwise.

## Quick Reference

- **When/where:** 2281, 204 years after the Great War, 4 years after Fallout 3, 40 years after Fallout 2. The
  Mojave Wasteland around the surviving city of Las Vegas ("New Vegas") and Hoover Dam.
- **Why the place matters:** Vegas was barely nuked (Mr. House's defences stopped most warheads), and Hoover
  Dam still generates power and controls clean water. It is the richest prize in the post-war West.
- **Premise:** you are a courier, shot in the head and buried over a poker-chip-shaped data key (the Platinum
  Chip). You survive, hunt the man who shot you, and discover the chip decides who rules the region.
- **Structure:** a revenge western for act one, a four-way political thriller for acts two and three. The
  revenge plot is deliberately small; it is the delivery mechanism for the political plot.
- **Four endings:** New California Republic (annexation), Caesar's Legion (conquest), Mr. House (autocratic
  city-state), or Independent Vegas via the obedient AI "Yes Man" (the Courier in charge).
- **No ending is clean.** Every faction is written with a real argument and a real rot. The slides tell you
  what your choice cost.
- **Continuity with Fallout 1/2:** the NCR is the grown-up version of Shady Sands; the Great Khans, the
  Followers of the Apocalypse, the Brotherhood, the Enclave, super mutants (Marcus) and Van Graffs all return,
  two centuries of consequences later. Much of the design descends from Black Isle's cancelled "Van Buren".
- **Reputation, not morality:** each faction and town tracks fame and infamy separately. Karma exists but
  matters little. Wearing faction armour disguises you.
- **Yes Man is a structural failsafe:** he cannot be permanently killed, so the main quest is always
  completable even if the player has murdered every other quest-giver.
- **28 state-driven ending slides** between an intro and a conclusion (29 numbered slides in total), most
  narrated by a character from the community concerned.
- **Companions:** 8 (six humanoid, two non-humanoid; one of each at a time), each tied to a faction's history
  and each with a personal quest that changes their ending slide.
- **DLC arc:** four linked stories about people who cannot let go of the past, tied together by a second
  courier, Ulysses, who is foreshadowed in three add-ons and met in the fourth.
- **DLC one-liners:** Dead Money = greed and letting go; Honest Hearts = faith, vengeance and mercy; Old World
  Blues = nostalgia for a future that never happened; Lonesome Road = unintended consequences and the death
  and birth of nations.
- **No post-game.** The game ends at Hoover Dam. Post-ending play and more Legion territory east of the
  Colorado were planned and cut; development was roughly 18 months.
- **Tone:** dry, political, funny in the margins, rarely wacky in the centre (wackiness is opt-in through the
  "Wild Wasteland" trait and concentrated in Old World Blues).
- **Core theme:** the post-apocalypse is over; the question is what gets built next and which old mistakes
  get rebuilt with it.

## 1. Setting

### 1.1 Date and place

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Year | 2281 (the Courier is shot in October 2281; exact day `[UNVERIFIED]`, commonly given as 11 October, waking 19 October) |
| Region | Mojave Wasteland: southern Nevada with slivers of California and Arizona; bounded east by the Colorado River |
| Hub | New Vegas: the walled Strip, the slum of Freeside around it, and outer districts (Westside, North Vegas, South Vegas ruins) |
| Strategic asset | Hoover Dam: electricity and water for the whole region |
| Player level cap | 30 in the base game, +5 per story add-on to 50 |

### 1.2 How the world got here

Vegas survived the Great War of 2077 because Robert House, founder of RobCo and the richest man in the
region, predicted the war and built a missile-defence network run from his casino tower, the Lucky 38. Per
fallout.wiki, 77 warheads targeted the area; his systems disarmed 59 and lasers shot down 9 more, with the
rest landing outside the city. The software upgrade that would have made the defence complete was on the
Platinum Chip, which was in transit when the bombs fell and was lost for two centuries.

House spent decades in a coma after an emergency reboot, then watched the desert through cameras. When NCR
scouts reached Hoover Dam he woke his Securitron robots, recruited three local tribes, dressed them up as
pre-war casino cultures (the "Three Families"), and rebuilt the Strip before the NCR army arrived so that he
could negotiate from possession.

### 1.4 State of the world

- **The west is civilised and tired.** The NCR is a state of hundreds of thousands with taxes, a senate,
  cattle barons, a paper currency that lost its gold backing, and a president elected on military glory. It
  has overreached by a thousand kilometres of supply line.
- **The east is ordered and enslaved.** Caesar has forged dozens of tribes (he says 86; fallout.wiki's
  summary gives 87 `[DISPUTED]`) into one slave army with no identity but his.
- **The middle is a city run as a business** by a 260-year-old man in a life-support pod.
- **Everyone else** (locals, tribes, ghouls, mutants, small traders) is deciding who to fear least.

### 1.5 How it follows from Fallout 1 and 2

| Thread from the originals | Where it ends up in New Vegas |
|---|---|
| Shady Sands / NCR (FO1 village, FO2 republic) | A sprawling, bureaucratic, expansionist nation; the main "good government with bad habits" faction |
| The Khans (FO1 raiders), New Khans (FO2) | Great Khans: a proud, dwindling drug-running tribe nursing three generations of grievance against the NCR |
| Followers of the Apocalypse (FO1) | Underfunded anarchist-humanitarian medics in Freeside; also, accidentally, Caesar's teachers |
| Brotherhood of Steel | A defeated, isolationist chapter hiding in a bunker, losing a war of attrition against its own code |
| The Enclave (FO2) | Five pensioners with power armour in storage (the Remnants) and a doctor who hides his parentage |
| Super mutants; Marcus of Broken Hills (FO2) | Jacobstown, a mountain refuge for mutants; nightkin schizophrenia from Stealth Boy use |
| Van Graff family, Crimson Caravan, Gun Runners | NCR corporate interests extending into the Mojave |
| Vault experiments | Vaults 3, 11, 19, 21, 22, 34: each a finished tragedy to read, not a home to leave |
| Van Buren (cancelled Black Isle Fallout 3) | Source of Caesar's Legion, the Burned Man, Hoover Dam as a setting, Big MT ideas and more, heavily reworked. Avellone has said the Van Buren Legion would have differed significantly |

## 2. Protagonist and Premise

### 2.1 The Courier

- A package courier for the Mojave Express, one of six hired to carry decoy and real items to the Strip. The
  Courier ("Courier Six") carries the real one: the Platinum Chip.
- Deliberately a near-blank slate. No vault, no family, no fixed age, sex or origin. Unlike the Fallout 3
  protagonist there is no childhood prologue. Lonesome Road retroactively supplies one piece of history (see
  section 9.4), and Avellone stresses that even there "the motivations for your character are your own."
- The job was first offered to another courier, Ulysses, who withdrew when he saw the Courier's name on the
  list. This is a base-game detail (told by the Primm office manager) that pays off only in the last add-on.

### 2.2 The opening

Benny, head of the Chairmen (the family running The Tops casino), has learned what the chip is with help
from a reprogrammed Securitron, Yes Man. He hires Great Khans to ambush the Courier near the village of
Goodsprings, takes the chip, shoots the Courier in the head and buries them. One of House's Securitrons,
Victor, digs the Courier up; the town doctor patches them back together. Character creation is framed as
the doctor's medical tests.

### 2.3 The Platinum Chip

| Aspect | Detail |
|---|---|
| What it is | A pre-war data storage device shaped like a casino chip, made of platinum |
| What it does | Carries the upgrade for House's systems: Securitrons go from police-grade to military-grade (missiles, grenade launchers, self-repair) and the Lucky 38's defences improve |
| The hidden prize | A vault of dormant Securitrons under the Legion's own camp at Fortification Hill. Whoever upgrades or destroys that army decides the war |
| Who wants it | House (it is his), Benny (to replace House using Yes Man), Caesar (wants what is under his hill destroyed), and eventually the Courier |
| Cost | House says he spent hundreds of thousands of caps over years finding it (in-game figure 812,545 `[UNVERIFIED]`) |

## 3. Main Plot Synopsis

### 3.1 Act one: the trail (linear in story, open in geography)

| Beat | Quest | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ain't That a Kick in the Head | Wake in Goodsprings. Character creation. Released into the world |
| 2 | Back in the Saddle / By a Campfire on the Trail | Optional tutorial with a local hunter. First moral choice: defend Goodsprings from Powder Gangers hunting a caravan guard (Ghost Town Gunfight) or help the gang take the town (Run Goodsprings Run) |
| 3 | They Went That-a-Way | Follow Benny's trail south and around: Primm (convict-occupied; learn the Khans' route), Nipton (first sight of the Legion: a town crucified by lottery), Novac (a sniper knows where they went), Boulder City (the Khans are cornered by NCR; one hands over proof that Benny is on the Strip) |
| 4 | Ring-a-Ding-Ding! | Enter the Strip (credit check of 2,000 caps, a forged passport, or other routes). House summons the Courier to the Lucky 38 and hires them to recover his chip. Confront Benny at The Tops: kill him, be seduced or seduce, be tricked, or let him flee to the Legion. Meet Yes Man in Benny's suite |

The road is a loop on purpose: the direct northern route to Vegas is blocked by deathclaws and giant
wasps, so first-time players are funnelled past every faction's opening statement before reaching the city.

On leaving The Tops the Courier is approached by both the NCR (invitation to the embassy) and the Legion
(a token of safe passage from Caesar, with all past offences pardoned once). Both faction reputations are
reset at this point so that every route is open regardless of act-one behaviour.

### 3.2 Act two pivot: the Fort

Whichever employer the Courier favours, the chip leads to Caesar's camp. Caesar asks the Courier to go into
the pre-war bunker beneath his weather station and destroy whatever House has hidden there. Inside is the
Securitron army. The Courier can upgrade it (for House or for themselves) or destroy it (for Caesar or the
NCR), and can lie about which they did. Benny, if he fled, is a prisoner here and can be executed, made to
fight, or quietly freed.

### 3.3 The four paths

All four routes run through the same set of "tribes to be dealt with", which is where side quests feed the
main plot (section 8). Each route has warning quests that fail you out of a rival route once you have gone
too far against that faction.

#### Path A: New California Republic

| Step | Quest | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Things That Go Boom | The ambassador sends the Courier to win over (or neutralise) the Boomers at Nellis |
| 2 | Kings' Gambit | Deal with the Kings' harassment of NCR citizens in Freeside: broker peace or remove their leadership |
| 3 | For the Republic, Part 2 | Colonel Cassandra Moore's task list: end the Great Khan-Legion alliance (or the Khans), stop the Omertas' plot, destroy the Brotherhood (a truce is possible with the ambassador's backing if the right Elder leads), investigate and then kill Mr. House |
| 4 | You'll Know It When It Happens | Protect President Kimball during a morale visit to the dam from several assassination methods (sniper, bomb on his aircraft, disguised killers) |
| 5 | Eureka! | Second Battle of Hoover Dam. Fight east across the dam, enter the Legate's camp, defeat Legate Lanius by force or persuade him his conquest cannot be held |

**Outcome:** NCR annexes the Mojave and the Strip. Order, taxes, and resentment follow.

#### Path B: Caesar's Legion

| Step | Quest | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Render Unto Caesar | Destroy House's bunker army, kill House, secure or eliminate the Boomers, destroy the Brotherhood, and tie off allies (Khans, Omertas) |
| 2 | Et Tumor, Brute? | Caesar has a brain tumour. Cure him (repair his surgical machine, or operate with high Medicine, or bring a doctor), let him die, or botch it deliberately. If Caesar dies, Lanius inherits |
| 3 | Arizona Killer | Assassinate President Kimball at the dam |
| 4 | Veni, Vidi, Vici | Storm the dam from the east, reach General Lee Oliver's compound, kill him or frighten him into retreat |

**Outcome:** the Legion takes the dam and Vegas. With Caesar alive: brutal but orderly occupation. With
Caesar dead: Lanius rules with sharper cruelty.

#### Path C: Mr. House

| Step | Quest | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | The House Always Wins I-II | Recover the chip, go to the Fort, upgrade the Securitron army |
| 3-5 | The House Always Wins III-V | Win over the Boomers; expose the Omertas; destroy the Brotherhood of Steel. The last is not negotiable: House regards them as an irrational threat to his technology |
| 6 | The House Always Wins VI | Keep Kimball alive (House wants the NCR humiliated in the field, not martyred) |
| 7-8 | The House Always Wins VII-VIII | Install an override at a power substation so the dam can energise the buried army |
| 9 | All or Nothing | Battle. Power up the army from the dam control room, deal with Lanius, then face General Oliver and tell the NCR to leave |

**Outcome:** House evicts both armies with his robots, keeps the NCR as paying customers, and runs Vegas as
an efficient, cold autocracy. His stated long game is to use Vegas revenue to restart high technology and
eventually spaceflight within decades.

#### Path D: Independent Vegas (Yes Man)

| Step | Quest | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wild Card: Change in Management | Kill or disconnect House in his life-support chamber (disconnection is a slow death sentence for him) and install Yes Man in the Lucky 38 mainframe |
| 2 | Wild Card: You and What Army? | Upgrade the bunker army for yourself (optional; skipping it changes the ending) |
| 3 | Wild Card: Side Bets | Yes Man lists every minor faction; for each the Courier decides to ally, ignore or destroy. Nothing is mandatory |
| 4 | Wild Card: Finishing Touches | Kimball's fate is optional; install the substation override |
| 5 | No Gods, No Masters | Battle as in the House path, but the Courier dictates terms. General Oliver can be talked down or thrown off the dam by a robot |

**Outcome:** Vegas is independent under whatever the Courier is. With the upgraded army the city is held
and stable; without it, the slides describe disorder. Yes Man closes by announcing an "assertiveness"
update to himself, which players have read as either a patch against future reprogramming or a threat
`[DISPUTED]`; Obsidian staff have indicated the benign reading.

### 3.4 The final battle, common shape

1. Briefing with your faction's leader. Point of no return (the game says so).
2. Fight along the dam; allies you earned appear (Boomer bomber overhead, Remnants in power armour, Khans,
   Rangers, Kings and Followers tending wounded, and so on).
3. Confront the enemy commander: Legate Lanius (for NCR, House, Independent) and/or General Oliver (for
   Legion, House, Independent). Both can be defeated by speech at very high skill.
4. Ending slideshow. No return to the world.

## 5. Factions

### 5.1 The four contenders

| Faction | Goal | Ideology | Genuine strengths | Genuine flaws |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **New California Republic** | Annex the Mojave, keep the dam, eventually absorb Vegas | Constitutional democracy, rule of law, old-world American values | Real courts, schools, trade, a professional army, civil rights (including for women and, on paper, ghouls and mutants). Most individual soldiers and officers are decent | Overextended; run for the benefit of cattle and farm barons; taxes without services on the frontier; annexes rather than allies; committed Bitter Springs; a glory-hunting general and a jingoist president; treats locals as obstacles |
| **Caesar's Legion** | Take the dam, make Vegas the Legion's Rome, then absorb the NCR | Totalitarian militarism modelled on Rome; individuals have value only as parts of the state. Caesar frames history as Hegelian dialectic: NCR as thesis, Legion as antithesis, a new synthesis after conquest | Its territory is reported as the safest in the wasteland for traders; no raiders, no corruption, no chems; absolute discipline; it gave warring tribes peace. Caesar is educated, lucid, and a persuasive critic of the NCR | Slavery; women as property; crucifixion as policy; rejection of medicine and technology; erasure of every culture it touches; entirely dependent on one dying man with a far worse successor; an economy of conquest that needs new victims |
| **Mr. House** | A sovereign Vegas under his sole control, funded by NCR tourism, as the seed of a technological rebirth | Technocratic autocracy; enlightened self-interest; contempt for democracy and for nostalgia | He actually saved the city; he has a 200-year record of competence, a plan measured in decades, and no appetite for cruelty for its own sake | Answers to no one; indifferent to Freeside and everything outside his walls; demands the Brotherhood's extermination; treats people as assets; there is no succession because he does not intend to die |
| **Independent Vegas** | Whatever the Courier wants | None supplied. Benny's stolen plan, executed by an AI that cannot refuse | No foreign master; every local faction can be spared; the player's own values rule | No institutions, no legitimacy but force; stability depends on a robot army and on one unelected person; slides stress uncertainty. Arguably the House ending with a less qualified dictator |

### 5.2 Minor factions

### 5.3 How "both strengths and flaws" is actually done

- **Testimony from outsiders.** The Legion's best argument is never made by a legionary first; it comes from
  a neutral trader and from a companion who has lived in Legion Arizona. The NCR's worst is told by its own
  veterans (Boone, Hanlon, the Bitter Springs staff).
- **Named decent people inside each side** (NCR officers trying to feed refugees; a Legion-side slave who
  argues she is safer than before) and **named rot inside each side** (a mole, a corrupt quartermaster, a
  colonel who prefers extermination to treaties).
- **Leaders argue their case in person at length**, and the player may challenge them. Caesar and House both
  get long, quotable, internally consistent interviews.
- **Asymmetry is allowed.** The game does not pretend the Legion is as defensible as the NCR. Sawyer has
  acknowledged the Legion is under-shown because its home territory was cut; what remains is mostly its
  military face. Writers and critics still debate whether this unbalances the choice `[DISPUTED]`.

## 8. Side Quests and the Reputation Web

### 8.1 How reputation works

- Each faction and town tracks **fame** and **infamy** independently; they do not cancel. Helping and
  harming the same group yields mixed standings rather than neutrality.
- Thresholds unlock or lock quests, prices, safehouses, hit squads, and dam-battle allies.
- **Disguises:** faction armour makes you count as that faction to most observers.
- **One-time amnesty** from NCR and Legion after act one keeps all four paths reachable.
- **Fail-forward:** killing a quest-giver fails quests rather than ending the game; Yes Man always remains.
- **Karma** is tracked separately and only alters the Courier's own ending slide and a few reactions.

### 8.3 The web in one sentence

The "tribes" (Boomers, Khans, Brotherhood, Kings, Followers, families, Remnants) are each a self-contained
side story whose resolution is then read three times: by your main-path employer (who may demand a specific
outcome), by the battle (who shows up), and by the slides (what became of them under the winner you chose).

## 9. The Story Add-ons

### 9.1 Summary table

| Add-on | Released | Place | Antagonist | Central theme | Signature constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dead Money** | 21 Dec 2010 | Sierra Madre casino and its poisoned villa | Father Elijah | Greed; letting go; beginning again | All gear confiscated; explosive collar; toxic cloud; radio signals that detonate the collar; enemies that must be dismembered |
| **Honest Hearts** | 17 May 2011 | Zion Canyon, Utah | Salt-Upon-Wounds and the White Legs (and Graham's own wrath) | Faith, vengeance versus mercy, paternalism toward "innocent" peoples | Carry-weight limit entering; open wilderness, light plot |
| **Old World Blues** | 19 Jul 2011 | Big MT research crater | Dr. Mobius, then the Think Tank themselves | Obsession with the past and with a future that never came; science without ethics | The Courier's brain, heart and spine are removed; comedy tone |
| **Lonesome Road** | 20 Sep 2011 | The Divide | Ulysses | Consequences of careless acts; what makes a nation; history | Linear road; no companions but a robot; nuclear launch decision |

Each raises the level cap by 5. Dead Money and Old World Blues were led by Chris Avellone; Honest Hearts by
Josh Sawyer; Lonesome Road by Avellone.

### 9.2 Dead Money

**Backstory.** A pre-war tycoon, Frederick Sinclair, built the Sierra Madre as a fortress-casino to shelter
the singer he loved, Vera Keyes, from the coming war. He learned she and the entertainer Dean Domino were
conspiring to rob it, redesigned the vault as a trap for them, then repented too late. The casino never
opened; its security holograms, vending machines and a corrosive red cloud have guarded it ever since. Its
construction workers, sealed in hazard suits, became the near-unkillable "ghost people". A radio invitation
has lured treasure hunters for two centuries. The casino's legend is that it offers a chance to begin again.

**Plot.** The Courier follows the broadcast to an abandoned Brotherhood bunker, is gassed, and wakes in the
villa wearing a bomb collar. Father Elijah, speaking through a hologram, explains the rules: assemble a
team of three other collared captives, trigger the casino's gala opening, and break the vault. If one
collar-wearer dies, all die.

| Team member | Who | Their obsession | Possible ends |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dog / God** | A nightkin with two personalities: a ravenous servant and a controlling intellect | Hunger and obedience versus control | One personality destroyed, or the two merged; or killed |
| **Dean Domino** | Ghoul lounge singer, the original conspirator, waiting 200 years to finish his heist | Envy and entitlement | Survives only if the Courier never bruised his ego; otherwise turns on them |
| **Christine Royce** | Brotherhood assassin hunting Elijah; mute after being trapped in a surgical machine; Veronica's lost lover | Revenge | Can be left to guard the Madre, or attempt to kill Elijah |

Inside, each companion's story resolves separately, then the Courier descends to the vault. Elijah's plan is
to use the cloud, the holograms and the collars to depopulate the Mojave and rule the silence. The Courier
can kill him, trap him in the vault with his prize, or (with specific prior conditions) join him in a
non-standard ending. The vault holds 37 gold bars, each too heavy to carry more than a few of; escaping
with the collar counting down means leaving most of the fortune behind.

**Theme.** Every character, including the dead ones in terminals, is ruined by being unable to release
something. The gold-bar weight is the theme turned into a mechanic. Avellone describes the design goals as
isolation, claustrophobia and greed, and a wish to make ordinary healing items feel precious again.

**Reception note.** The most divisive add-on on release for its difficulty and restrictions; later
re-evaluated as the most thematically unified.

### 9.3 Honest Hearts

**Setup.** The Courier joins a caravan to re-establish trade with the Mormon settlement of New Canaan. It is
ambushed in Zion; the Courier alone survives.

**Peoples of Zion.**

| Group | Nature | Leader / guide |
|---|---|---|
| **White Legs** | Raiders from the Salt Lake who destroyed New Canaan to earn admission to the Legion | Salt-Upon-Wounds |
| **Dead Horses** | Outsider tribe allied to the New Canaanites | Joshua Graham; the young scout Follows-Chalk |
| **Sorrows** | Peaceful natives of Zion, descended from children who survived there | The missionary Daniel; the midwife Waking Cloud |
| **New Canaanites** | Post-war Latter-day Saint community, now refugees | Graham and Daniel |

**The choice.** Daniel wants to evacuate the Sorrows and abandon Zion so they need never learn to kill.
Graham wants to arm both tribes and exterminate the White Legs. Both are outsiders deciding for the tribes.
After gathering supplies, the Courier backs one plan. If fighting, the last decision is whether Graham
executes the beaten enemy chief or is persuaded to spare him.

| Outcome | Result in the slides |
|---|---|
| Evacuate | Sorrows keep their innocence and lose their home; Zion falls to the White Legs; Daniel is haunted |
| Fight, with mercy | White Legs broken; Graham finds some peace; Sorrows become more warlike but tempered |
| Fight, no mercy | Graham sinks back toward the Legate he was; the tribes inherit his ruthlessness |

**The Survivalist.** A trail of terminals left by Randall Clark, a pre-war soldier who lived in Zion's caves
for decades, records his family's death, his war against invaders from Vault 22, and his secret guardianship
of the children who became the Sorrows, who remember him as a deity. Widely regarded as the add-on's best
writing; it is environmental storytelling that explains the present culture.

**Theme.** Whether violence in a just cause damns you; whether sparing people the truth is protection or
control; faith treated without mockery.

### 9.4 Old World Blues

**Setup.** A crashed satellite projecting a midnight film abducts the Courier to Big MT (the "Big Empty"), a
pre-war research crater. The Courier wakes without a brain, heart or spine, replaced by machinery. The
perpetrators, five scientists who long ago became brains in floating robot bodies (the Think Tank: Klein,
Dala, Borous, 0 and 8), say the brain has been taken by their exiled colleague Dr. Mobius.

**Plot.** Explore the crater's laboratories to collect three technologies, build up a home base whose
appliances all have personalities, storm Mobius's dome, and find a gentle, drug-addled old scientist who
reprogrammed his colleagues to forget the outside world exists, because if they ever left they would
experiment on it. The Courier then negotiates with their own brain (which rather likes its tank) and returns
to the Think Tank to stop them from leaving, by persuasion or force.

**Why it matters to the wider canon.** Big MT is revealed as the origin of much of the Mojave's misery:

| Mojave / add-on horror | Big MT origin |
|---|---|
| Cazadores and nightstalkers | Bred here by Dr. Borous and escaped |
| Vault 22's spores | Related botanical research |
| Sierra Madre's cloud, holograms, vending machines, hazard suits | Tested on the Sierra Madre as a field site |
| Bomb collars | Used on prisoners in the crater's internment camp |
| Lobotomised wanderers | Earlier abductees |

**Theme.** The title names the condition: being so fixed on what was, or on the gleaming future the past
promised, that you cannot see the present. Avellone's phrasing of the subject is "the optimistic atomic
future of what might have been", with the fault lying in the thinking and not in the technology. The tone
is 1950s B-movie farce, but the scientists are monsters who have forgotten they were ever people.

### 9.5 Lonesome Road

**Setup.** Ulysses sends a message inviting the Courier to the Divide to hear why he refused the Platinum
Chip job.

**Backstory revealed.** Before the game, the Courier's deliveries had opened a route through the Divide and
a community grew along it; Ulysses, then a Legion scout, saw in it the beginning of a nation not built from
old-world leftovers. The NCR moved in. The Courier then delivered a package from the old Enclave base in the
west: a device that woke and spoke to the dormant missiles under the Divide. The detonations tore the land
open. NCR troops and Legion soldiers caught there survive as flayed, radiation-sustained "Marked Men", still
fighting side by side. The Courier never knew.

**Ulysses.** Born to a tribe (the Twisted Hairs) that scouted for Caesar and was then enslaved and erased by
him. He served the Legion as a spy, saw the NCR's bear and the Legion's bull as dying symbols, armed and
trained the White Legs, passed through Big MT, and pointed Elijah toward the Sierra Madre. He wears the
old American flag as a question, not an allegiance.

**Plot.** A long descent through missile silos, a collapsed highway and a ruined city, accompanied by a
copy of ED-E, with Ulysses talking over the radio. At the final silo Ulysses intends to launch on the NCR's
supply line. The Courier can kill him or, with high speech or evidence gathered along the road, convince him
to stand down, after which he fights alongside against the Marked Men.

| Final choice | Effect |
|---|---|
| Stop the launch | ED-E sacrifices itself; nothing is destroyed |
| Launch on the NCR | The Republic's supply road is hit; a new irradiated zone opens in the base game |
| Launch on the Legion | The Legion's staging ground is hit; likewise |
| Launch on both | Both; both factions turn hostile |

**Theme.** One person's unthinking act can make or unmake a people. Avellone has said the add-on was built
around the closing image of the first Fallout (a lone figure walking away), here inverted into walking
into your own past. He has also said the nuclear option was meant as a way to reset a setting he felt the
NCR was making "too civilized", and that he views both NCR and Legion as failed ideas worth clearing away
for something new. That authorial position is contested among fans and was not shared by all of the base
game's writers `[DISPUTED]`.

| **Base game hooks** | Veronica knows Elijah and Christine | Legion and caravan talk of the Burned Man | A drive-in satellite on the map from day one | The Primm clerk's story of the courier who refused |

Avellone confirms the arc was planned once four add-ons were approved, to create "a larger narrative arc
rather than a series of isolated adventures". Note that the popular "letting go" label for the whole arc is
drawn from Dead Money's text and from critics and interviewers; in the interviews consulted Avellone does
not himself use it as the name of the arc.

## 10. The Ending-Slide System

### 10.1 Mechanics

- Triggered only by completing one of the four final-battle quests. There is no post-game.
- An intro, 27 conditional subject slides, and a conclusion. Slides for communities are mostly narrated by
  a character from that community; the frame is narrated by the series' usual narrator.
- Each slide checks a short priority list: **death of the key figure(s) overrides everything**, then the
  local quest outcome, then which of the four finales was completed. Many slides are therefore a small
  matrix of local choice x winner.
- A "did nothing" variant exists for most subjects, so neglect is also a recorded choice.
- Each add-on has its own shorter slideshow at its own end.

### 10.3 The four headline endings

| Victor | Trigger quest | Headline consequence | Typical knock-on in other slides |

| House | *All or Nothing* | Both armies expelled; Strip secure; Freeside ignored | Outlying towns left to themselves; Brotherhood necessarily destroyed |

### 10.4 What the system achieves

- It is the game's actual ending; the battle is the punctuation.
- It rewards breadth: a player who resolved many local stories gets a long, personal epilogue.
- It enforces the thesis that no victor is good for everyone: each of the four winners produces several
  slides that sting.
- Its weakness, acknowledged by the developers' unrealised plans, is that the player reads about the new
  world and never walks in it.

## 11. Themes and Tone

| Theme | How it is expressed |
|---|---|
| **Rebuilding versus repeating** | Every faction copies a dead model: the NCR pre-war America, the Legion Rome, House 1950s Vegas, the Kings a pop singer they do not understand, the Brotherhood itself. The question is always whether imitation of the past can build a future |
| **Letting go** | The add-ons make it explicit; the base game runs it through Boone, Veronica, Raul, the Khans, the Remnants |
| **Greed and the gamble** | The casino setting, the Platinum Chip, the Sunset Sarsaparilla "treasure", the Sierra Madre vault. Sawyer wanted greed and excess, from real Vegas history, at the centre |
| **Power and legitimacy** | Election, conquest, ownership, or a gun and a robot army: four theories of the right to rule, none endorsed |
| **Nations and symbols** | Flags, the bear and the bull, the uniform as disguise; Ulysses's whole monologue |
| **Imperialism** | The NCR "liberating" people who did not ask; the Legion assimilating tribes; Daniel and Graham deciding for the Sorrows |
| **Unintended consequences** | The Followers educate Caesar; the NCR's rail project arms convicts; the Courier destroys the Divide by doing their job |
| **Western genre** | A man left for dead, a trail, frontier towns needing sheriffs, cavalry and tribes, a showdown; then the genre's assumptions are questioned |

**Tone.** Drier and more grounded than Fallout 3. Violence has political context. Humour is in dialogue
and side characters, not in the premise; the opt-in Wild Wasteland trait quarantines the series' sillier
jokes. Sexuality and vice are present matter-of-factly. The writing trusts players to sit through an
argument with a slaver-king and reach their own verdict.

**Critical consensus.** Reviews at launch (Metacritic 84/82/84 across PC/PS3/360) praised writing and quest
design and criticised bugs and engine age. Its reputation has risen steadily; it is now commonly ranked
among the best of the series, mainly for reactivity and faction writing.

## Design Takeaways

**Learn from**

- **Make the opening a small personal grievance that hands the player a large public question.** Revenge
  gets the player moving; by the time it resolves they care about the region.
- **Route the first journey past every faction's thesis statement.** The southern loop is a guided tour
  disguised as an open world. Soft-gate with danger, not walls.
- **Give every faction an advocate who is not a member, and a critic who is.** Credibility comes from
  hostile witnesses and loyal dissenters.
- **Let the villain talk, at length, and be right about something.** Then let the player disagree with
  actions, not just dialogue.
- **Minor factions as modular stories read three times** (by the employer, by the finale, by the epilogue).
  This is the cheapest high-reactivity structure in the genre and we should copy the structure outright.
- **Fame and infamy as separate tracks.** Mixed reputations feel truer than a single slider and create
  interesting middle states.
- **Always keep one path the player cannot break.** A failsafe route lets every NPC be killable without
  dead-ending the story. Design ours to be a real choice, not just a safety net.
- **A one-time amnesty** at the act break keeps early experimentation from locking out late options.
- **Companions as embodied arguments** about the world's factions, each with a quest whose two outcomes
  are both defensible.
- **Epilogue slides with "you did nothing" variants.** Neglect should be a visible choice.
- **Mechanics that state the theme** (gold too heavy to carry out). One such device per major story beat is
  worth more than pages of dialogue.
- **Linked expansions with a foreshadowed figure** build anticipation cheaply: a name in the base game, a
  trace in each add-on, a meeting at the end.
- **Finished tragedies as dungeons.** The best vaults are places where the story is over and the player
  reconstructs it. Environmental narrative scales well.

**Avoid**

- **Do not ship a major faction whose virtues are only hearsay.** If one side's case depends on territory
  the player cannot visit, the choice tilts. Budget the homeland of every contender or cut the contender.
- **Do not end the world at the climax if the game is about what gets built.** Plan the post-victory state
  from the start, even if small: one changed hub is better than none.
- **Do not let the "free" ending be free of institutions and of cost.** An independence path needs its own
  politics, or it becomes the default power fantasy.
- **Do not let a late expansion's author overrule the base game's thesis.** A reset button that erases the
  rebuilding everyone just argued over undercuts the argument. Decide early whether our world is allowed to
  recover, and hold to it.
- **Do not retrofit a history onto a blank-slate protagonist** unless the player can contest it.
- **Do not split hubs into loading cells late.** Decide crowd density and district scale against target
  hardware before content is built.
- **Do not rely on an invisible morality meter alongside reputation.** One system that matters is better
  than two where one is vestigial.
- **Do not copy names, factions, iconography or text.** The transferable material is structural: the
  four-way contest, the tribe modules, the slide matrix, the hostile-witness technique.
