# Fallout 2 (1998, Black Isle Studios) — Lore and Canon Reference

Scope: the story, world, factions, characters, endings, tone, and later-canon status of Fallout 2. Mechanics
are mentioned only where they carry narrative weight. Confidence tags follow `research/CONVENTIONS.md`.

## Quick Reference

- **Released** 30 October 1998 (gold master 22 October), Black Isle Studios / Interplay. Built in roughly
  9-11 months on the Fallout 1 engine with staff borrowed from Planescape: Torment.
- **Set in 2241-2242**, 80 years after Fallout 1 and 164 years after the Great War, across northern
  California, southern Oregon and western Nevada.
- **Protagonist: the Chosen One**, grandchild of Fallout 1's Vault Dweller, from the tribal village of Arroyo.
- **Premise:** Arroyo is dying of drought; the Chosen One must find a Garden of Eden Creation Kit (GECK), a
  pre-war terraforming kit issued to Vaults.
- **The twist:** the GECK hunt is the first two-thirds. When the Chosen One returns, Arroyo has been abducted by
  the Enclave, and the game becomes a rescue and a strike on an offshore oil rig.
- **Antagonist: the Enclave**, the self-perpetuating remnant of the pre-war US government, which regards
  everyone on the mainland as mutated and plans to exterminate them with an airborne FEV derivative.
- **Final boss: Frank Horrigan**, a mutated Secret Service agent sealed in power armor. He cannot be talked
  down, a deliberate contrast with Fallout 1's Master.
- **The world has moved from survival to politics.** Three expanding powers (New California Republic, Vault
  City, New Reno) compete for the gold town of Redding and for each other.
- **Central theme: what kind of civilisation gets rebuilt.** Each town is a different answer: democracy with
  imperial habits, technocratic slave state, mob rule, integrated mining town, pure-blood fascism.
- **Quests interlock across towns.** A power-plant repair in one town decides the fate of two; a mining chip
  sold in Redding decides which power absorbs it.
- **About 14 permanent companions**, including a super mutant sheriff, a ghoul doctor, an intelligent
  deathclaw and a teenage drug chemist.
- **Ending is a slideshow of roughly 13 community epilogues** (about 45 variants) narrated by Ron Perlman, then
  free play continues.
- **Tone is far broader than Fallout 1**: overt pop-culture jokes, talking animals, a ghost, kung-fu duels, a
  Scientology parody, a porn studio. This is the game's most persistent criticism.
- **Each area had a different designer and little central oversight** (Chris Avellone later called it
  "slapdash"), which explains both its richness and its tonal whiplash.
- **Later canon keeps the spine** (GECK found, oil rig destroyed 2242, Enclave broken, NCR keeps growing) and
  leaves most town outcomes vague.
- **Biggest retcons applied to it:** the Enclave was not the last of the US government; Jet was not invented in
  2241; talking deathclaws were written out.

## 1. Production Context (why the lore looks the way it does)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Developer / publisher | Black Isle Studios / Interplay |
| Release | 30 Oct 1998 (shipped to stores 26 Oct) |
| Schedule | Announced by Tim Cain Dec 1997 as an 11-month project; Feargus Urquhart later said the team effectively had "nine months to make the whole game" |
| Leads | Feargus Urquhart and Matt Norton (lead design), Gary Platner (art), Urquhart and Eric DeMilt (production) |
| Departures | Tim Cain, Leonard Boyarsky and Jason Anderson left during development and founded Troika; they had laid down the initial story arc |
| Borrowed designers | Chris Avellone, Colin McComb, Zeb Cook, Eddie Rainwater (from Planescape: Torment) |
| Known area authorship | Avellone: New Reno and Vault City. Other attributions not verified here `[UNVERIFIED]` |
| Composer / narrator | Mark Morgan / Ron Perlman |
| Reception | Metacritic 86; RPG-of-the-year nominee at several outlets, losing to Baldur's Gate each time |
| Sales | ~123,000 US copies by March 2000 |

The brief was "twice the size of Fallout 1". Designers drafted whole regions and moved on; artists and
programmers filled empty space with whatever amused them (GameBanshee retrospective, citing programmer Dan
Spitzley). Marketing mandated a tutorial, which became the widely disliked Temple of Trials. Scott Everts and
Avellone have both said the game was too big and under-supervised. Keep this in mind for every tonal
complaint below: the inconsistency is structural, not a single authorial choice.

### 2.2 How the world changed in 80 years

- **From scavenging to economies.** Fallout 1's towns survived on pre-war leftovers. Fallout 2's towns
  produce things: gold (Redding), uranium (Broken Hills), drugs and vice (New Reno), medicine (Vault City),
  brahmin beef and law (NCR), power (Gecko), slaves (the Den).
- **Currency changed.** Bottle caps are gone; the NCR mints gold-backed coins, which is why Redding's gold
  matters to everyone. `[RETCON]` later games bring caps back.
- **States exist.** Shady Sands, a mud-brick village in Fallout 1, is now the capital of a republic the game
  credits with about 700,000 citizens across five states (Shady, Los Angeles, Maxson, Hub, Dayglow).
- **The Master's army dissolved.** Surviving super mutants scattered; some integrated (Broken Hills), some
  became raiders, a new batch was accidentally made at Mariposa.
- **Ghouls dispersed** from Necropolis after the mutant attack and founded Gecko.
- **The Brotherhood of Steel has shrunk into irrelevance.** It keeps small, nearly empty outposts in the Den,
  NCR and San Francisco and has lost its technological lead to the Enclave.
- **Tribal cultures have appeared.** Descendants of vault dwellers and survivors have, in three generations,
  become spear-carrying villagers with shamans, ancestor spirits and face paint. This was criticised as
  implausibly fast.
- **Organised vice and slavery are mainstream industries**, not raider side-activities.
- **Old-world technology is being rediscovered rather than merely found**: a drivable car, cybernetic dogs,
  combat implants, a functioning AI, a tanker, a (never launched) space shuttle.

## 3. Protagonist and Premise

**The Chosen One** is a young Arroyo villager, grandchild of the Vault Dweller and child of the village Elder
(father unrecorded). The tribe treats the Vault Dweller as a founding saint and Vault 13 as a holy place; the
old Vault suit and Pip-Boy are relics.

**Arroyo** is a small farming-and-hunting tribal village in coastal southern Oregon, reached across a rope
bridge. In 2241 the crops fail, the brahmin sicken and children are dying. The Elder and the shaman Hakunin
decide that only a GECK, described in the holy "Vault-Tec" disks, can save them. The Chosen One proves
themself in the **Temple of Trials** (a trap-and-ant-filled ruin ending in a duel, or a talk-down, with the
warrior Cameron), receives the Vault 13 jumpsuit, a Pip-Boy, a little money and a water flask stamped "13",
and is told to find the trader Vic, who sold such flasks.

**The GECK** as presented: a briefcase-sized kit of seeds, fertiliser, a cold-fusion power source and
instructions, intended to help a Vault restart agriculture. It is closer to a very good toolkit than magic
in Fallout 2 and the Bible. `[RETCON]` Fallout 3 turned it into near-instant matter-rearranging terraforming.

**Pressure device:** Hakunin appears in escalating dream sequences, each showing the village sicker. There is
no real deadline (the game hard-ends only after 13 in-game years), but the dreams create felt urgency.

## 4. Main Plot Synopsis, Beat by Beat

1. **Opening film.** A cheerful Vault-Tec instructional reel on leaving the Vault cuts to a Vault door
   opening and armoured soldiers gunning down the people who walk out. The player does not yet know these are
   the Enclave at Vault 13.
2. **Temple of Trials and departure.** See above.
3. **Klamath.** A trapper town. Vic is gone; his shack holds a radio and his trail leads to the Den. A crashed
   vertibird in the canyon is the first physical trace of the Enclave. The tribal Sulik can be freed from debt.
4. **The Den.** Vic is held by the slaver boss Metzger, who wants him to fix a radio. Freed by payment,
   persuasion or massacre, Vic says the flasks came from a trader in Vault City.
5. **Vault City.** A walled city built by Vault 8 with its GECK, now used up. Getting inside requires a day
   pass, forged papers, citizenship by test, or doing the First Citizen a favour (fixing the leaking ghoul
   reactor at Gecko). The Vault's records point to Vault 15, and through it to Vault 13.
6. **Scripted Horrigan sighting.** Somewhere on the road the player stumbles on Frank Horrigan and Enclave
   troopers executing a family of wastelanders. The player can only watch.
7. **Middle game, free order.** The player crosses Modoc, Gecko, Redding, New Reno, Broken Hills and reaches
   the NCR. Two main routes find Vault 13:
   - **Vault 15 route.** President Tandi asks the player to deal with squatters and the raiders (the New
     Khans under Darion) occupying Vault 15. Darion's computer holds Vault 13's coordinates.
   - **Westin route.** Councilman Westin's brahmin are being raided; solving it yields the Vault 13 location.
8. **Vault 13.** It is inhabited by intelligent, peaceful deathclaws led by Gruthar, an escaped Enclave
   experiment, sheltering a handful of humans. The main computer needs a voice-recognition module. With it
   fixed, the player takes the Vault's spare GECK. (Vault 13 had two because of a shipping mix-up with
   Vault 8, which got Vault 13's spare water chips; this retroactively explains Fallout 1's premise.)
9. **Arroyo is gone.** Hakunin's final vision is of the village burning. On returning, the bridge is cut, the
   village is wrecked, and the dying Hakunin says men in flying machines took everyone south. He points the
   player to a ship in San Francisco.
10. **(If revisited) Vault 13 massacre.** The Enclave returns and slaughters the deathclaws to remove the
    evidence. The good outcome is nearly unreachable in the shipped game `[DISPUTED: trigger is buggy]`.
11. **San Francisco.** The tanker PMV Valdez is beached at the docks, occupied by vagrants. To sail it needs
    three things: **fuel** (from the Shi, or from the Hubologists, or by having the tanker's hacker reroute
    it), a **navigation computer part** (found at Vault 13 or Navarro), and a **tanker FOB** (from the Navarro
    base commander).
12. **Navarro.** An Enclave air base hidden behind a derelict fuel station. The player can bluff in as a new
    recruit with a password, be screamed at by a drill sergeant, steal vertibird plans and advanced power
    armor, and take the FOB by theft, persuasion or force.
13. **The Oil Rig (Control Station Enclave).** The tanker's autopilot takes the player to a Poseidon Energy
    platform off the coast. Inside: the kidnapped Arroyo villagers and Vault 13 dwellers in holding cells,
    most of them already test subjects. The player learns the plan (section 7) from President Richardson
    himself, who explains it calmly to anyone in an Enclave uniform.
14. **Destroying the rig.** The reactor must be overloaded to break the force fields holding the prisoners.
    Three routes: persuade chief scientist Dr Charles Curling of the project's monstrosity so he releases the
    toxin into the rig's own ventilation; have a technician or the player's Science skill sabotage the
    coolant system; or destroy the reactor computer with explosives. Richardson can be killed or
    pickpocketed for his access key.
15. **Frank Horrigan.** He blocks the only exit. No dialogue option avoids the fight. Help is available: a
    squad under Sergeant Granite can be convinced to turn on him, and the room's turrets can be reprogrammed
    with the presidential key. Bisected, Horrigan triggers a self-destruct with his last words.
16. **Escape and epilogue.** The tanker leaves as the platform goes up in a nuclear fireball, killing the
    President and the Enclave's leadership. Villagers and Vault 13 dwellers together found a new community
    with the GECK. Slides follow; then the player is returned to the world to keep playing.

### 5.2 Location notes

**Klamath.** The tutorial town after the tutorial. Establishes the "two families, one honest and one not"
pattern, the low-intelligence dialogue mode (via the simple-minded herder Torr), and Sulik, a tribal with a
bone through his nose who consults "Grampy Bone" and is searching for his kidnapped sister. A side canyon
holds the vertibird wreck and a hostile robot. Trapper Town's rat warren ends with a giant talking-point
rat, Keeng Ra'at.

**The Den.** A rotting town of two streets. Metzger's guild runs slave pens openly; the player can join a
slaving run (earning a permanent forehead tattoo and the Slaver reputation, which locks out several
companions and the Rangers) or wipe the guild out. Other threads: tavern-keeper Becky undercutting Frankie's
bar; gang leader Lara's turf war over a church; orphan pickpockets fenced by a local shopkeeper; the ghost
of Anna Winslow asking for her locket to be buried; a refugee who tells the Master's story. Smitty sells the
**Chryslus Highwayman**, a nuclear car that needs a part from Gecko. The Den is where the game announces its
adult register.

**Modoc.** A farm town dying of the same drought as Arroyo. Mayor Jo suspects something evil at the "Ghost
Farm", where bodies are staked in the fields. The bodies are dummies; the farmers are the **Slags**, pale
underground people who want trade and are terrified of the surface. The player can broker peace (and must
find a missing villager, Karl, alive in the Den to prove nobody was murdered), fail, or help Modoc massacre
them. Smaller threads: a missing boy; a stolen watch that turns out to have been taken by a rat; the
butcher Grisham, whose son or daughter the player can sleep with, leading to a literal shotgun wedding and a
useless spouse-companion (the wedding is available to either sex, an early same-sex marriage in games).

**Vault City.** The game's most developed political portrait. Vault 8 was a control Vault that opened on
schedule (2091 per the Bible) and used its GECK perfectly. The result is clean, safe, medically advanced,
smug, xenophobic and built on "Servants" who are slaves in all but name. Citizenship is by birth or by an
exam designed to be failed. First Citizen Joanne Lynette is one of the series' great unlikeable authority
figures. The city has outgrown its Vault generator and refuses to deal fairly with Gecko's ghouls, whose
reactor both contaminates its water and could solve its power problem. Vice-leader-in-waiting Councillor
McClure is the pragmatist. Courtyard outsiders (the bar owner Cassidy, a forger, a drunk farmer) show what
exclusion costs.

**Gecko.** A ghoul shanty around a pre-war reactor. Harold, the ancient mutant from Fallout 1 with a tree
growing from his head, runs the plant. Options: repair the reactor with a part pried out of Vault City;
additionally optimise it so it yields surplus power; or sabotage it into meltdown, killing the town. The
ghouls' economic study showing mutual benefit can be carried to McClure. Beneath the town, "the Brain", a
hyper-intelligent albino mole rat with a ghoul cult, plans species-wide domination and wants the same
outcome for his own reasons.

**Redding.** A Western mining town. Sheriff Earl Marion has a bad leg and deputises the player for a run of
frontier jobs (bar brawl, a cut prostitute, an eviction, the Frog Morton gang). Mayor Ascorti sells the
player the Great Wanamingo Mine, infested with "wanamingos" (tentacled, Alien-like creatures). Clearing it
yields an excavator chip that only one of two rival owners can use: **Marge LeBarge** (Kokoweef Mine,
pro-NCR) or **Dangerous Dan McGrew** (Morningstar Mine, pro-New Reno). The miners are being hooked on Jet;
Doc Johnson wants an antidote, which is Vault City's lever.

**New Reno.** The game's showpiece and the most reactive hub. No government, no police. Four families:

| Family | Boss | Base | Business | Outside link |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mordino | Big Jesus Mordino (son: Little Jesus) | Desperado casino; the Stables lab | Jet, slaves, prostitution | Addicting Redding; buys from Den slavers |
| Bishop | John Bishop (wife Leslie Anne, daughter Angela) | Shark Club | Gambling, mercenaries | Secret pact around Vault City's raiders and the NCR; wants NCR figures killed |
| Salvatore | Louis Salvatore | Salvatore's Bar | Protection, laser weapons | Secret desert trades with the Enclave |
| Wright | Orville Wright | Family mansion | Alcohol; the weakest and the only real "family" | Wants the Sierra Army Depot's weapons |

The player can work for all four up to the point of being a **Made Man** for one, which turns the others
hostile. Threads: the Wright son's fatal overdose (a whodunit with four suspects; the Salvatores were behind
it); Myron, the teenage inventor of Jet, recruitable from the Stables; a boxing ladder ending in a
ear-biting champion; a porn studio; a weapons dealer with a savant upgrader in the basement; a cemetery
where the player can find their own stolen car's thieves; a drunk priest. Sleeping with either Bishop woman
can leave an heir who appears in the ending.

**Sierra Army Depot.** A sealed pre-war arsenal north of New Reno, entered by firing a howitzer at the gate.
Robots, forcefields, an organ-harvesting lab, and **Skynet**, an AI that wants a body and will join the
party in a robobrain if given the right brain.

**Broken Hills.** Proof that integration can work, and that it is fragile. Marcus, a former soldier of the
Master, is sheriff. A human conspiracy plots to sabotage the mine's air purifier and kill the mutants; a
mutant, Francis, has been murdering humans and sealing the bodies underground. Lighter content sits on top:
a chess-playing radscorpion, a talking plant, an arm-wrestling mutant, an outhouse-explosion quest.

**NCR.** The player meets it at its capital, a walled, policed town with a statue of the Vault Dweller in
the square. Weapons must be holstered, slavery is illegal inside and practised at the front gate, and the
Rangers run an abolitionist underground. President **Tandi**, rescued as a girl in Fallout 1, is near 100
and still in office. Vice-President Carlson and Councilman Westin represent factional politics; both can be
assassinated on Bishop's orders. Dr Henry, an Enclave defector, builds cyber-dogs. A crime boss, a
Hubologist recruiter and an unstable policeman at the power plant round it out.

**Vault 15.** Tandi's ancestral Vault. Squatters on the surface live under the thumb of the New Khans in the
levels below, led by Darion, the last survivor of the raider gang the Vault Dweller wiped out in Fallout 1,
obsessed with Tandi. An NCR insider is feeding him information. Clearing it and brokering NCR's aid makes it
NCR territory.

**Vault 13.** The Fallout 1 home, now a sanctuary. Gruthar's deathclaws are gentle, literal-minded and
moral; Goris, a scholarly one in a robe, joins the party. A human saboteur hides among them.

**Military Base.** Optional. The collapsed Mariposa of Fallout 1, re-dug by the Enclave. The vats are
intact; the mutants left behind are hostile; the mutant magician Melchior conjures creatures as a boss.

**San Francisco.** Three powers and a ship. The **Shi**, descended from the crew of a Chinese submarine,
run Chinatown under an "Emperor" that is in fact a computer, and are doing serious research (fuel synthesis,
plant biology). The **Hubologists**, a transparent Scientology parody with celebrity members, intend to fly
a derelict space shuttle to their star-gods. The **tanker vagrants** are punks living on the Valdez. The
**Brotherhood outpost** asks for vertibird plans, as do the Shi and the Hubologists, a three-way fetch that
decides who the player sides with. Two martial artists, Lo Pan and the Dragon, each want the other beaten
unarmed. The area was visibly rushed.

**Navarro.** Played partly as military farce (drill-sergeant tirade, bored mechanics) and partly as
foreshadowing. Holds K-9, a cyber-dog disabled for refusing unethical orders, and Xarn, a caged intelligent
deathclaw.

**Oil Rig.** Corridors, labs, a presidential office, barracks, a reactor, holding cells. Cold, clean and
bureaucratic, deliberately unlike the Master's organic Cathedral.

### 5.3 Cut locations `[SEMI-CANON]`

Planned but cut: the **Abbey** (scholar-monks preserving knowledge), the **EPA** (a pre-war research
facility), a **primitive tribe** village (Sulik's home; his sister's storyline is unresolved in the shipped
game), a **village outside Vault City**, and Hubologist ending slides. Fans restored versions in the
unofficial Restoration Project. None of it is canon.

**Reading the faction map.** North: Vault City and Gecko locked in a dependency neither admits. Centre:
Redding, the prize. East: New Reno, a parasite on Redding and the Den. South: the NCR, pushing north. The
NCR-Bishop arrangement is the hidden hinge: raiders paid through Bishop make Vault City frightened enough to
accept NCR "protection", and Bishop gets recognition in return. Evidence found in the raiders' cave (an
account book, a holodisk) exposes it. The Enclave sits outside the whole system, touching it only through
the Salvatore trades, Navarro and its kidnappings.

## 7. The Enclave as Antagonist

### 7.1 Who they are

Descendants of the politicians, generals, corporate officers and scientists who retreated to a Poseidon
Energy oil platform before the bombs. They kept the offices and rituals of the United States (a President,
a Vice-President, a Secret Service, elections of a sort) with none of the population. They also, the
President reveals, commissioned the Vaults as **social experiments**, never as shelters: Vault 13 was to
stay closed 200 years as a study in prolonged isolation, Vault 15 mixed radically different cultures, Vault
8 was a control group. Fallout 2 introduces this idea in dialogue; the Fallout Bible expanded it into a
list, and every later game leaned on it.

### 7.2 The plan

1. Recover FEV from Mariposa (2236), using enslaved mainlanders as diggers.
2. Refine it into **FEV Curling-13**, a toxin lethal to anyone carrying mutation, meaning everyone exposed
   to the post-war world.
3. Test it on an uncontaminated control group (Vault 13's dwellers) and a contaminated one (Arroyo's
   villagers, chosen because the Enclave found them while tracing Vault 13 descendants).
4. Inoculate the Enclave, release the toxin into the jet stream, wait, then reclaim the continent.

### 7.3 Key figures

| Figure | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Dick Richardson | President | Genial, reasonable in manner, wholly sincere. Cannot be persuaded. |
| Daniel Bird | Vice-President | A babbling, addled figurehead; political satire. |
| Dr Charles Curling | Chief scientist | Author of the toxin; the only senior figure who can be argued into doubt. |
| Frank Horrigan | Secret Service enforcer | Born 2211; mutated at Mariposa; a 12-foot cyborg who calls everyone else a mutant. |
| Sergeant Granite | Rig guard | Can be turned against Horrigan. |
| Sergeant Dornan | Navarro drill instructor | Comic relief. |
| Dr Schreber | Navarro scientist | Vivisects intelligent deathclaws. |
| Dr Henry | Defector, in NCR | Proof that people do leave. |

### 7.4 The Enclave versus the Master

| Axis | The Master (Fallout 1) | The Enclave (Fallout 2) |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | One composite, suffering being | An institution; replaceable office-holders |
| Aim | Make everyone a mutant to end division | Kill everyone "mutated" to restore purity |
| Use of FEV | Transformation | Extermination |
| Self-image | Saviour of the future | Rightful owners of the past |
| Flaw | Factual: his mutants are sterile | Moral: the premise is the crime; nothing to disprove |
| Can be talked down | Yes; he kills himself when shown the evidence | No. The President shrugs; Horrigan only fights |
| When introduced | Rumours from early on, a face late | Seen in the intro and on the road, named very late |
| Aesthetic | Flesh, corridors of tissue, cult | Steel, uniforms, paperwork |
| Irony | A monster with a humane argument | Humans with a monstrous one; their champion is the biggest mutant in the game |

**Assessment.** The two are deliberate mirror images, and the Enclave's point is that the old world's
government is the thing the wasteland least needs back. As drama the Enclave is weaker: it has almost no
presence in the first two-thirds, its ideology is not debatable, and a game celebrated for talk-your-way
solutions ends in a compulsory boss fight (softened by the turret and squad options). As a setting element
it was the more durable invention; the series reused the Enclave repeatedly and never found another Master.

## 9. Side Quests and How They Interlock

The design lesson of Fallout 2 is the cross-town chain. The main ones:

| Chain | Towns | Shape | Decides |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The reactor** | Vault City, Gecko | Lynette demands the ghoul problem be "solved". Get a part from Vault City, repair; optionally optimise; optionally carry the economic study to McClure. Or cause a meltdown. | Both towns' endings; citizenship |
| **The raiders** | Vault City, raider cave, New Reno, NCR | Clear the raiders, find the books, trace the money to Bishop, discover the NCR angle, carry diplomatic holodisks between Lynette and Westin | Whether Vault City stays independent or is absorbed |

## 11. Themes and Tone

### 11.1 Themes

- **Reconstruction, not survival.** Often called the "post-post-apocalypse". The question is who gets to
  define the new normal.
- **Competing blueprints.** NCR (law and expansion), Vault City (order and exclusion), New Reno (pure
  market), Broken Hills (pluralism), the Shi (science), tribes (tradition), Enclave (restoration by
  genocide). None is clean; the NCR is the least bad and is still shown hiring raiders.
- **Purity and prejudice.** Vault City against outsiders, humans against mutants in Broken Hills, everyone
  against ghouls, townsfolk against "tribals", and the Enclave as the logical end point. The Chosen One is
  on the receiving end throughout.
- **Economic conquest.** Redding is taken by debt, drugs or contracts, not armies.
- **Slavery and exploitation** as the foundation of otherwise civilised places.
- **The old world as villain.** The pre-war government is not mourned; it is the final boss.
- **Legacy.** The Vault Dweller is a statue, a saint and a set of half-wrong stories; Tandi, Harold and
  Marcus let the player measure 80 years.

### 11.2 Tone and humour

Fallout 1 kept its jokes dry and its overt references mostly inside rare random encounters. Fallout 2 puts
them in the main path. A sample, described not quoted:

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Parody factions | A Scientology stand-in with celebrity members; mobsters in pinstripes with tommy guns |
| Named homages | An AI called Skynet; martial artists borrowed from kung-fu cinema; a mine boss named after a Planet of the Apes character; a megalomaniac lab-rodent |
| Random encounters | Knights seeking a holy hand grenade; a bridge-keeper with riddles; a crashed sci-fi shuttle; a fallen whale and flowerpot; a time portal that lets the player cause Fallout 1's water-chip failure; a café of out-of-work Fallout 1 characters |
| Setting-stretchers | Talking deathclaws, a talking plant, a chess-playing scorpion, a ghost, a mutant who conjures monsters, shamanic dreams that are literally true |
| Adult comedy | Porn-star career, prostitution quests, a shotgun wedding, drug humour |
| Fourth-wall breaks | NPCs commenting on the game, its bugs and its designers |

Darkness sits right beside it: child addicts, slave pens, a roadside family execution, vivisection, and the
ability to kill children (removed from some European releases).

### 11.3 Criticism

- **Too many references, too overt.** The most repeated complaint, from fans and from original lead Tim
  Cain, who has argued that Fallout 2 "went overboard" and that real-world references should be invisible
  to anyone who does not already know them (paraphrased from secondary summaries of his video commentary;
  primary video not consulted `[UNVERIFIED]`).
- **Tonal inconsistency.** Area-by-area authorship means Vault City's sober politics and San Francisco's
  kung-fu cult farce share a map. Avellone called the project "slapdash".
- **Setting drift.** Period-correct retro-futurism gave way to 1990s real-world guns, 1930s gangsters and
  mysticism. The tribal premise is thin given the three-generation gap.
- **Weak opening and rushed ending.** The Temple of Trials punishes non-combat builds in a game about build
  freedom; San Francisco and the rig are thin; the final fight is mandatory.
- **A late, flat villain** (section 7.4).
- **Bugs.** Severe at launch, including the car's trunk detaching and several ending triggers failing.

### 11.4 What was praised

Scale and freedom; New Reno's reactivity; cross-town quest chains; reputations; better companions; writing
quality at its peaks (Vault City, New Reno, the NCR-Bishop plot); a world that feels like it has politics.
It is routinely ranked among the best RPGs ever made, and New Vegas is in most respects a direct descendant.

## Design Takeaways

**Learn from**

- **Give every settlement an export, an import and a grievance.** Fallout 2's map reads as a world because
  towns need each other. Design the trade graph before the quests.
- **Make one contested neutral prize.** Redding turns three abstract powers into a concrete choice with a
  single item. Build at least one such hinge per region.
- **Let quests cross borders and change two endings at once.** The reactor chain is the template: one
  problem, two communities, four or five outcomes, with the best one requiring a non-obvious extra step.
- **Make "more help" able to go wrong.** Optimising Gecko's plant without the diplomacy enslaves the ghouls.
  Outcomes that follow from power shifts, not from a morality meter, feel earned.
- **Hide strength counters behind faction endings.** New Reno's winner is whoever the player left strongest,
  which rewards every small action without a dialogue choice labelled "pick a winner".
- **Permanent, visible reputations.** Titles that NPCs react to (and that close doors) do more for
  role-playing than a karma number.
- **Use the sequel gap.** Eighty years let returning characters and places show consequence. Decide early
  what our world looks like one or two generations on.
- **A villain that mirrors the previous one** gives a series thematic continuity. The inversion (forced
  unity versus enforced purity) is worth studying; the institution-as-villain idea is strong.
- **Bait-and-switch the quest goal** after the player has succeeded, not before. Finding the GECK and then
  losing the village lands because the player completed the stated mission first.
- **Free play after the ending** with slides first is a cheap, well-loved feature.

**Avoid**

- **Jokes that only work if you know the reference.** Our humour should come from the world's own logic. If
  we include oddities, quarantine them in optional encounters or behind a toggle.
- **Region-by-region authorship without a tone bible.** Parallel area design is efficient; it needs one
  editor with veto power and a written list of what the setting does not contain.
- **Supernatural elements played straight** in a setting sold on pseudo-science, unless we commit to them
  from the start.
- **A villain who is absent until the last act and immune to the game's core verbs.** If speech is a
  build, the finale must honour it. Seed the antagonist's fingerprints in every hub.
- **A mandatory tutorial that contradicts character freedom.** Teach inside the first real town.
- **Scope set by rivalry.** "Twice as big" produced an unfinished final third and broken ending flags.
  Budget the last hub first.
- **Endings whose best variant is unreachable or obscure by accident.** Test every slide trigger; if a good
  outcome needs a precise sequence, signpost it in fiction.
- **Parody of real organisations and people.** Legally risky, dates fast, and it shrinks the world.
- **Fixing fine-grained canon in out-of-game documents.** The Fallout Bible created as many disputes as it
  settled. Keep our lore source of truth in one maintained place and mark what is deliberately open.
