# Designer Lessons: What the Creators Say They Learned

Purpose: an organised digest of statements by Fallout's designers about their own work, for use on an ORIGINAL post-apocalyptic game.

Research limits (important): web search quota was exhausted early, and the sites that host most primary Sawyer/Avellone/Pagliarulo material (Formspring/Tumblr archives, IGN, RPS, Eurogamer, GDC Vault, YouTube transcripts) were not fetchable. Verified material here comes mainly from the notes of Cain's GDC 2012 talk, Game Developer, Destructoid, Wikipedia and fallout.wiki. Entries marked `[UNVERIFIED - from memory of public talks]` are widely repeated positions that I could not re-confirm in this session; treat them as leads to check, and do not quote them. All quotes below are short and taken from fetched pages; where the fetched page paraphrased a speaker I say so.

## Quick Reference

- Cain: mood and rules documented in a vision statement let Fallout survive the loss of GURPS and a two-week rewrite.
- Cain: each quest should be solvable at least two of three ways (fight, talk, sneak). His line on player agency: he does not mind if people play badly, he wants them to live with the consequences.
- Cain: jokes should be invisible to players who do not get them, so the game does not date.
- Cain: dislikes morality meters; prefers grey-area quests. (Bethesda later added Karma, a visible meter.)
- Cain: his one named regret is the shipped timed water-chip quest, removed by a launch-day patch.
- Cain: moral questions over weapons: "explore more of the world and more of the ethics of a post-nuclear world, not to make a better plasma gun."
- Boyarsky: do the apocalypse as 1950s movies imagined it: science both causes and rescues. Vault Boy and the retro-future give a distinctive, instantly readable identity.
- Avellone: Fallout 2 was uneven because designers inherited unfinished designs and a very short schedule; he called it "a slapdash project without a lot of oversight".
- Avellone: companions are written as characters with their own agendas (Ulysses, Cassidy); his Fallout Bible was meant "just for fun", not scripture.
- Sawyer (verified): left Van Buren after losing faith; on NV he tuned every weapon and creature by hand.
- Sawyer (leads to confirm): skill thresholds that gate dialogue rather than random rolls; faction design with no "right" side; Hardcore mode as optional survival; levels that guide with landmarks. All `[UNVERIFIED]` here.
- Howard: V.A.T.S. was "Burnout crash mode with body parts"; he prefers FO1's humour to FO2's pop-culture jokes; he wants "complete freedom" (GTA V cited).
- Howard on 76: regretted no beta; "It's not how you launch, it's what it becomes."
- Pagliarulo: "canon always starts with what is in the games"; case-by-case use of the Bible; put real places from his life in FO4; keep-it-simple quest writing `[UNVERIFIED]`.
- Common thread: people-first design. Scenes, factions and consequence beat feature lists.

## 1. Tim Cain

### 1.1 Vision, scope and survival

| Lesson | What he says / what happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Write a vision statement | The team only gelled once a mission statement existed; it was written while still on GURPS and outlived the licence loss | Game Developer; GDC notes |
| Small budget keeps the project alive | In 1994 Cain argued for a small budget to stop Interplay cancelling it | GDC notes |
| Custom technology has costs | Not a typical Interplay game because it had its own engine and no famous licence | Game Developer |
| Egoless team | He described the team as hard-working and all aiming at the same game | GDC notes |
| Not meant as a franchise | "We didn't sign up to make a franchise." | fallout.wiki (Fallout 2) |

### 1.2 SPECIAL

- The system replaced GURPS in about two weeks after Steve Jackson Games withdrew; perks were added just after at Fargo's request (implemented in a day by Chris Taylor).
- Naming: his own acronym idea was rejected; SPECIAL was suggested by someone else (GDC notes; Destructoid gives a variant spelling, so the exact name of the rejected acronym is [DISPUTED]).
- Fallout 1 was designed as "wide, not deep": short, but with very different outcomes depending on choices (fallout.wiki, paraphrasing).
- Cain, later reviewing Fallout 3, praised Bethesda's adaptation of SPECIAL into first-person and its grasp of lore, but criticised its humour and reuse of story elements (Wikipedia, paraphrase).

### 1.3 Tone

- Humour must not date the game and must be optional: "If the player doesn't get them, they shouldn't notice them." Pop-culture jokes (a later Fallout 2 habit) violate this.
- Tone sources: A Canticle for Leibowitz, Road Warrior, The Day After, Ink Spots songs from the 1940s-50s; Vault Boy "cheekiness" of not taking things too seriously.
- Ambient score was meant to be noticed mainly when absent; marketing called it depressing.
- Called shots produced dark humour (especially against robots) without being scripted.

### 1.4 Time limit

- The original had a timed water-chip quest that staff argued about for months. A release-day patch removed the timer. Cain says if he could change one thing it would be removing that timed quest. (GDC notes.)
- Takeaway: hard real-time pressure on a non-linear exploration game punishes the play style the game invites.

### 1.5 Player agency and consequence

- "I don't mind if people play badly, I just want them to live with the consequences if they do." (GDC notes, paraphrased from the talk).
- Each quest needed at least two of three approaches: fight, talk, sneak.
- He disliked morality systems and preferred grey-area quests.
- The game tracked consequences in both the world and the player, which he credits as influential on later RPGs (D&D 3e, Oblivion, Skyrim, WoW talent trees, per his own account).

### 1.6 What he would change

- Remove the timed quest (stated).
- On Fallout Tactics: "My idea is to explore more of the world and more of the ethics of a post-nuclear world, not to make a better plasma gun." (fallout.wiki)
- On sequels: marketing influence took part of the game away: "We were losing part of the game to a larger group who had bigger plans for it." (Wikipedia)

## 2. Leonard Boyarsky (art and tone)

| Point | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Founding pitch | "What if we do post-apocalyptic worlds the way that movies in the 1950s thought it would happen?" (as given on fallout.wiki) | fallout.wiki/Fallout |
| Retro-future | Science caused the disaster and offers rescue; 13-star flag, Vault Boy cards, and the Vault Dweller exile ending | fallout.wiki/Leonard_Boyarsky |
| Polish regret | He worked on keeping the player's feet from sliding and later said that effort might have been better spent elsewhere | fallout.wiki |
| Concept recycling | An unused Fallout 2 pitch (lobotomised creatures ambushing the player, helped by a renegade Brotherhood soldier) resurfaced in Old World Blues | fallout.wiki/Fallout_2 |

Takeaway: one coherent art idea (a past future) carries tone better than a long list of gritty assets.

## 3. Chris Avellone

- Fallout 2: area designer (New Reno, Vault City raider content) after the founders left. Verdict: uneven because designers were handed unfinished designs and a short schedule; "a slapdash project without a lot of oversight" (Wikipedia).
- Fallout Bible (2002): compiled for fans; said it was not scripture ("not supposed to start some religion", "This is just for fun" per fallout.wiki); later said it is no longer canon (2011).
- New Vegas: wrote companions Ulysses and Rose of Sharon Cassidy; Ulysses was cut as a companion for space and reused as a DLC antagonist. Led DLC (Dead Money, Old World Blues, Lonesome Road).
- Companion philosophy `[UNVERIFIED - from memory of public talks]`: each companion should push on the protagonist with a distinct view, with personal quests that react to your behaviour. Check against his interviews before citing.
- Fallout 2's excess of jokes: Howard and Pagliarulo and Cain all criticise it (see sections 1.3 and 5); Avellone's own comment was about the unevenness above. A direct Avellone quote on joke excess was not found [UNVERIFIED].

## 4. Josh Sawyer

Verified from fetched pages:

- Left Black Isle in November 2003: "It's happened enough to me recently that I lost faith." (fallout.wiki)
- Tried to narrow the scope of Avellone's Van Buren material; wrote the New Canaan and Burham Springs design docs.
- On New Vegas: project director and system designer; hand-tuned every weapon; re-tuned automatic weapons because they felt unsatisfying; credited with making Deathclaws as tough as they are.
- Wanted themes of greed and excess drawn from Las Vegas, "recreating the new world in the image of the old"; scouted the Mojave on a motorcycle.
- Describes New Vegas as a project with heavy overtime and crunch weekends (fallout.wiki), though the Wikipedia article reports 40-50 hour weeks [DISPUTED].
- Planned post-ending play and reactivity were cut for time; Legion territory east of the Colorado was dropped (Wikipedia).
- PS3's split memory pool was the largest technical problem, which also drove cuts.
- Later work: Pillars of Eternity (crowd-funded in 27 hours at his suggestion), Pentiment.

Positions widely attributed to him in talks and Q&As, needing confirmation `[UNVERIFIED - from memory of public talks]`:

| Topic | Position |
|---|---|
| Choice and consequence | Reactivity should be visible and proportionate; build choices around who the player is (skills, factions, reputation) |
| Skill thresholds | Use fixed skill thresholds so players can plan, and make high-skill options feel earned; avoid hidden random failures on dialogue |
| Faction design | Give each faction a coherent logic and real flaws; no "good" faction; let the player support any |
| Hardcore mode | Optional survival rules (food, water, sleep, ammo weight, limb damage) layered on a normal game |
| Level design | Guide by landmarks and sight lines, not invisible walls or hand-holding |
| Regrets | List of New Vegas regrets includes cut Legion lands, post-ending content, engine instability, and launch bugs; confirm against his own Q&As before relying |

## 5. Todd Howard and Emil Pagliarulo (Bethesda)

### Verified

- Howard preferred the humour of Fallout 1 to Fallout 2's pop-culture references and fourth-wall breaks (fallout.wiki/Fallout_3).
- V.A.T.S.: needed so first-person play kept RPG feel; "Burnout crash mode with body parts" (Howard).
- Pagliarulo: influences include post-apocalyptic films, 1950s sci-fi and Deus Ex; wanted stealth improved.
- Pagliarulo on canon: "canon always starts with what is in the games"; the Bible is used case by case: "It's a judgment call."
- Fallout 4: put real places from his life in the game (childhood house, high school, train station, sub shop) and voiced Parker Quinn.
- Howard on freedom: wants to "give you complete freedom"; GTA V as the benchmark (GameSpot, via fallout.wiki).
- Howard on 76: regretted not releasing a beta before launch; "It's not how you launch, it's what it becomes." (IGN interview via Wikipedia)
- Pagliarulo on Tranquility Lane: "It was okay for a kid to die in the simulation in the video game, because he wasn't a real kid." (Gamescom 2020) - an example of context shaping what is acceptable to depict.

### Leads to confirm `[UNVERIFIED - from memory of public talks]`

| Topic | Position |
|---|---|
| Environmental storytelling | Howard and Joel Burgess have described telling stories through arranged scenes in the world (skeletons, props), a technique very visible in FO3/FO4 |
| Dialogue wheel regret | Bethesda staff have discussed that FO4's four-option, voiced dialogue gave less nuance than text lists; the exact statements were not located |
| Voiced protagonist | Chosen for cinematic conversations and camera work; costs: shallow role-playing and fewer reactive lines |
| Quest writing | Pagliarulo argues for "keep it simple": clear hook, clear goal, a twist or choice, rather than long branching |
| Fallout 76 launch | Howard's regret about no beta is verified (above); other lessons need sourcing |

## Design Takeaways

- **Write the tone rules down.** "Jokes must be invisible to those who do not get them" is a rule a whole team can apply. Draft equivalent tone rules for our game before content starts.
- **Require multiple solutions per quest.** Cain's two-of-three rule (combat, talk, stealth) is cheap to audit and guarantees build variety.
- **Do not put real-time clocks on exploration.** The one thing Cain would change is the thing every designer since has avoided; use soft pressure (rising threat, shifting factions) instead.
- **Let players live with consequences.** Show effects through the world rather than through a visible morality score.
- **Give the art one idea.** A single reinterpretation (a past's future) beats a heap of gritty assets.
- **Scope companions to what you can finish.** Ulysses was cut for space; plan fewer, deeper companions.
- **Keep dialogue legible and reactive.** A voiced protagonist costs variety; if we voice, budget for reactivity and tone via text/options elsewhere.
- **Run a public beta before any live-service launch.** The clearest regret on record.
- **Verify before citing.** Several well-known Sawyer and Pagliarulo positions remain unconfirmed in this research; re-source them first.
