# Designer Lessons: What the Creators Say They Learned

Purpose: an organised digest of statements by Fallout's designers about their own work, for use on an ORIGINAL post-apocalyptic game. We study positions and numbers; we do not reuse Fallout names, text or art.

Research limits (current state): this file is now a digest layered on four evidence tiers. (1) Cain, Boyarsky and Avellone (sections 1-3) rest on GDC 2012 talk notes, Game Developer, Destructoid, Wikipedia and fallout.wiki, as before. (2) Sawyer (section 4) is now primary-sourced: his GDC 2012 slide deck, his Formspring Q&A archive 2010-2013 (searched word for word), and the JSawyer mod readme, all read in `sawyer-new-vegas-design.md`; the 2022-2026 remarks (GDS Prague, YouTube skill-check video, podcast) are secondhand press reports. (3) Bethesda (section 5) is mostly `[SECONDARY]`: the Bethesda.net pages are primary, but the Howard 2016 and 2025 and Pagliarulo 2023 and 2025 quotes come through aggregators because the original GameSpot, GQ UK, Polygon and GamesRadar pages could not be opened; wording matches across independent outlets, but re-check before any public citation. (4) Production numbers (section 6) carry the A/B/C grades from `production-metrics.md`. Still open: see section 8. Entries tagged `[UNVERIFIED]`, "tool-extracted" or "secondhand" keep that flag from the source file. Quotes are short, taken verbatim from the sibling files, and never reworded.

## Quick Reference

- Cain: mood and rules documented in a vision statement let Fallout survive the loss of GURPS and a two-week rewrite.
- Cain: each quest should be solvable at least two of three ways (fight, talk, sneak); he wants players to live with consequences, and his one named regret is the timed water-chip quest.
- Cain: jokes should be invisible to players who do not get them; he dislikes morality meters and prefers grey-area quests.
- Boyarsky: do the apocalypse as 1950s movies imagined it; one art idea carries tone better than a pile of gritty assets.
- Avellone: Fallout 2 was uneven because designers inherited unfinished designs on a short schedule ("a slapdash project without a lot of oversight").
- Sawyer on morality: Karma in New Vegas had "very little effect" (Formspring, 11 Jan 2011); per-faction reputation did the real work, and he agreed a reputation system beats a simple meter (5 Oct 2012).
- Sawyer on factions: he calls himself a "moral relativist"; the NCR/Legion plan (first read, then hidden dark side or virtue) failed for the Legion because its areas east of the Colorado were cut.
- Sawyer on kill policy: any visible non-child NPC can be killed and the critical path must still complete; the failsafe is the Yes Man route, and failed-quest messages are shown on purpose.
- Sawyer on checks: dialogue checks are visible flat thresholds because stand-alone random rolls just get reloaded; a failed check never leaves the player worse off.
- Sawyer's later caveat (2012, 2024): a visible unlocked option implies "you should pick it", which hurts role-playing; the GDC 2012 slide "Choice Conflict" is the same worry.
- Sawyer on distribution: spread checks over every skill and front-load them; Goodsprings was built so each tagged skill pays off (2024 video, secondhand).
- Sawyer on weapons: skill requirement sets the tier (0/25/50/75/100 = Tiers 1-5); mods and special ammo are vendor-only as a money sink.
- Sawyer on armor: Damage Threshold returned to keep light and medium armor viable; shipped floor is 20% of damage; Power Armor was "always" optimal in F1/F2.
- Sawyer's clearest named design failure: Charisma had no clear benefit, so it became a dump stat; "you probably screwed up" if players call a stat that.
- Hardcore Mode: meant as a combat and inventory challenge "without simply scaling hit points and damage", but his own verdict is that it is not "really" hard; the JSawyer mod (carry weight 150 to 50, doubled needs, scarce food and water) is his fix.
- World design: deathclaws near the start were deliberate soft gates; invisible walls were NOT absent - he says most should have been dropped. The earlier draft's "no invisible walls" claim was wrong.
- Production: New Vegas took about 18 months; the post-ending game, Legion camps, romances and Ulysses-as-companion were cut; "less content, fewer bugs would have been better."
- Bethesda dialogue: Howard 2016 "that didn't work as well"; Howard 2025 "it really did not resonate" and hard on designers. Nobody on record uses the word "regret" or "mistake" about the wheel (the word "mistake" is a GamingBolt headline).
- Bethesda voiced protagonist: Pagliarulo (Aug 2023) says a recorded voice was "too specific" for Starfield; the player-be-who-they-want goal required an unvoiced lead. Fallout 4 had two actors at 13,000+ lines each (Bethesda.net, verified).
- Actions speak: Far Harbor added choice, Wastelanders went back to list dialogue ("not limiting dialogue to just four options"), Starfield has an unvoiced, text-option lead.
- Howard on 76: regretted no beta ("It's not how you launch, it's what it becomes"); later "let a lot of people down" (reported 2021).
- Scope: only FO1 has a credible budget (about US$3M); New Vegas delivered about 65 voiced lines per person-month vs about 32 for FO4 (derived, grade C).
- Common thread: people-first design. Scenes, factions and consequence beat feature lists.

## 1. Tim Cain

### 1.1 Vision, scope and survival

| Lesson | What he says / what happened | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Write a vision statement | The team only gelled once a mission statement existed; it was written while still on GURPS and outlived the licence loss | Game Developer; GDC notes |
| Small budget keeps the project alive | In 1994 Cain argued for a small budget to stop Interplay cancelling it | GDC notes |
| Custom technology has costs | Not a typical Interplay game because it had its own engine and no famous licence | Game Developer |
| Egoless team | He described the team as hard-working and all aiming at the same game | GDC notes |
| Not meant as a franchise | "We didn't sign up to make a franchise." | fallout.wiki (Fallout 2) |

### 1.2 SPECIAL

- The system replaced GURPS in about two weeks after Steve Jackson Games withdrew; perks were added just after at Fargo's request (implemented in a day by Chris Taylor).
- Naming: his own acronym idea was rejected; SPECIAL was suggested by someone else (GDC notes; Destructoid gives a variant spelling, so the exact name of the rejected acronym is [DISPUTED]).
- Fallout 1 was designed as "wide, not deep": short, but with very different outcomes depending on choices (fallout.wiki, paraphrasing).
- Cain, later reviewing Fallout 3, praised Bethesda's adaptation of SPECIAL into first-person and its grasp of lore, but criticised its humour and reuse of story elements (Wikipedia, paraphrase).

### 1.3 Tone

- Humour must not date the game and must be optional: "If the player doesn't get them, they shouldn't notice them." Pop-culture jokes (a later Fallout 2 habit) violate this.
- Tone sources: A Canticle for Leibowitz, Road Warrior, The Day After, Ink Spots songs from the 1940s-50s; Vault Boy "cheekiness" of not taking things too seriously.
- Ambient score was meant to be noticed mainly when absent; marketing called it depressing.
- Called shots produced dark humour (especially against robots) without being scripted.

### 1.4 Time limit

- The original had a timed water-chip quest that staff argued about for months. A release-day patch removed the timer. Cain says if he could change one thing it would be removing that timed quest. (GDC notes.)
- Takeaway: hard real-time pressure on a non-linear exploration game punishes the play style the game invites.

### 1.5 Player agency and consequence

- "I don't mind if people play badly, I just want them to live with the consequences if they do." (GDC notes, paraphrased from the talk).
- Each quest needed at least two of three approaches: fight, talk, sneak.
- He disliked morality systems and preferred grey-area quests.
- The game tracked consequences in both the world and the player, which he credits as influential on later RPGs (D&D 3e, Oblivion, Skyrim, WoW talent trees, per his own account).

### 1.6 What he would change

- Remove the timed quest (stated).
- On Fallout Tactics: "My idea is to explore more of the world and more of the ethics of a post-nuclear world, not to make a better plasma gun." (fallout.wiki)
- On sequels: marketing influence took part of the game away: "We were losing part of the game to a larger group who had bigger plans for it." (Wikipedia)

## 3. Chris Avellone

- Fallout 2: area designer (New Reno, Vault City raider content) after the founders left. Verdict: uneven because designers were handed unfinished designs and a short schedule; "a slapdash project without a lot of oversight" (Wikipedia).
- Fallout Bible (2002): compiled for fans; said it was not scripture ("not supposed to start some religion", "This is just for fun" per fallout.wiki); later said it is no longer canon (2011).
- New Vegas: wrote companions Ulysses and Rose of Sharon Cassidy; Ulysses was cut as a companion for space and reused as a DLC antagonist. Led DLC (Dead Money, Old World Blues, Lonesome Road).
- Companion philosophy `[UNVERIFIED - from memory of public talks]`: each companion should push on the protagonist with a distinct view, with personal quests that react to your behaviour. Check against his interviews before citing.
- Fallout 2's excess of jokes: Howard and Pagliarulo and Cain all criticise it (see sections 1.3 and 5); Avellone's own comment was about the unevenness above. A direct Avellone quote on joke excess was not found [UNVERIFIED].
- New Vegas sidelight (US Gamer, 13 Sep 2017, secondhand): Avellone wanted players to "at least consider an alternate perspective" on the NCR. His NV design statements in the sibling sources are limited to this remark and DLC authorship.

## 4. Josh Sawyer

Every row below points to `sawyer-new-vegas-design.md` (abbreviated SNV) and its section number. Formspring answers are cited by the date stamped in the archive; "word for word" means the quote was verified against the downloaded archive text, "tool-extracted" means it passed through a summarising fetch tool and was not re-verified, "secondhand" means a press report of a talk or podcast I could not read directly. Scope lessons (PM s9 and takeaways):

1. Text-driven reactivity, skill-gated dialogue, faction reputation, ending slides and a written tone document are the cheapest high-value features; New Vegas shipped them on reused technology in 18 months.
2. Voice is the largest visible labour sink in the Bethesda games; Fallout 3 cut about 30% of its dialogue late for disc space. Make text the default and voice the exception.
3. Pick one expensive pillar (open world, voice, systemic building, or multiplayer) per game; FO4 combined several and FO76 combined a multiplayer retrofit, a large map and a fixed date.
4. Do not shrink the schedule while scope grows (FO2, NV, FO76 each shipped with visible damage); never launch networked content without a public beta.
5. Only the pre-Bethesda game has a stated budget, so record our own person-months, lines, quests and cuts from day one.

## 7. Cross-Designer Patterns

| Theme | Cain | Sawyer | Bethesda |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency | Multiple solutions; live with consequences | Kill-anyone rule with a Yes Man failsafe; per-faction reputation; visible flat skill thresholds | Freedom of exploration; Karma meter; Fallout 4 voiced lead with four paraphrase prompts, then list dialogue again in 76 and Starfield |
| Morality | Dislikes meters; grey-area quests | "Moral relativist"; Karma "very little effect"; reputation preferred | Karma kept as a visible meter in FO3 |
| Tone | Dark, mostly deadpan, jokes optional | Satire of excess, Western | Dark humour, retro-future, more silly in FO4 |
| Dialogue and checks | Talk is one of three solutions | Thresholds, "never ... worse off"; later worry that unlocked means "pick it" | Howard: "didn't work as well" (2016), "did not resonate" (2025); Pagliarulo: "too specific" |

## Design Takeaways

- **Write the tone rules down.** "Jokes must be invisible to those who do not get them" is a rule a whole team can apply. Draft equivalent tone rules for our game before content starts.
- **Require multiple solutions per quest.** Cain's two-of-three rule (combat, talk, stealth) is cheap to audit and guarantees build variety.
- **Do not put real-time clocks on exploration.** The one thing Cain would change; use soft pressure (rising threat, shifting factions) instead.
- **No global morality meter; track standing per faction.** Sawyer's verdict on Karma was "very little effect". Show consequences through who talks to you and what they refuse.
- **Fund every faction or cut one entirely.** The Legion failed Sawyer's own plan because its locations were cut at the map edge. Budget locations, a companion and quests per faction before alpha.
- **Let the player kill anyone, and write the failsafe.** Adopt the line-of-sight rule (children excepted), give the critical path a fallback route, and tell players what they locked out.
- **Use visible flat thresholds, never punish an attempt, and add a cost to some unlocked options.** Thresholds beat stand-alone rolls in a save-anywhere game; Sawyer's own 2012 and 2024 corrections say an unlocked option should not always be the right pick.
- **Audit skills and stats for use.** Front-load checks so every tagged skill pays off in the opening, count checks per skill, and treat any stat players call a dump stat as your bug (Charisma).
- **Pick one protection number.** Flat damage subtraction with a floor (NV used 20%) keeps light and medium armor viable; if a percentage layer is added, keep it small. Tie weapon tier to skill requirement.
- **Ship survival as real scarcity, not as a nuisance.** Shipped Hardcore was admittedly not "really" hard; start from low carry weight, scarce free water and healing, and doubled needs rates as in the JSawyer mod.
- **Gate with creatures, not walls.** A deadly optional area near the start teaches players to read the world. Avoid invisible barriers, start players away from the map edge, and plan landmarks.
- **Never hide the line in dialogue.** Show the words (or a faithful specific paraphrase) before commit; Bethesda's own staff and an eight-day fan mod both confirm the tone-label wheel failed. Option count follows the scene, not the controller.
- **Decide protagonist identity on day one.** A voiced, fixed-backstory lead (Fallout 4) and a blank-slate role-play promise conflict. Our default: unvoiced player, voiced world, exact-wording text options, and spend the voice budget on NPC reactivity.
- **Plan scope against the real schedule and build outward.** Fix scope early, build the mid-game first and the edge last so cuts happen at the edge, and prefer "less content, fewer bugs". Cut post-ending state in pre-production rather than late.
- **Run a public beta before any networked launch, and never retrofit multiplayer onto a single-player engine.** The clearest regret on record, with the worst Metacritic range (49-53) in the series.
- **Pick one expensive pillar per game and budget in person-months.** Copy New Vegas's economics (reused tech, text-first, about 65 voiced lines per person-month), not Fallout 4's.
