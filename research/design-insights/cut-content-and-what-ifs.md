# Cut Content and What-Ifs

Purpose: what the Fallout games cut or never built, why, and which unrealised ideas are worth learning from for an ORIGINAL post-apocalyptic game.

Research limits: search quota ran out; fallout.wiki pages for cut content usually list what exists in game files but rarely state reasons. Reasons below are marked as stated by developers, or `[INFERENCE]` where I am guessing from context. Items I could not confirm are `[UNVERIFIED]`.

## Quick Reference

- Fallout 1: unused locations include the Shrine, Brotherhood ruins, Burrows, Irvine; unused factions (Jackals, Rippers, Vipers, Thinker nightkin); several quests (Become a Blade, Save Trish, The Search for the Glow). Most are schedule casualties [INFERENCE].
- Fallout 2: cut includes a Hubologist shuttle interior, Shi submarine, mobile fortress, Den and Klamath quests; marketing's 16-bit graphics upgrade, full-auto mode and an expansion were considered and dropped.
- Van Buren: dual-mode combat, prisoner protagonist, race choice (human, ghoul, super mutant), mad scientist antagonist (Victor Presper), Southwest map. Cancelled; many setting ideas went to New Vegas.
- Fallout 3: downtown D.C. was twice as big at one stage; about 30% of dialogue cut late to fit the disc; five skills removed; White House never modelled.
- New Vegas: Legion territory east of the Colorado removed; post-ending play and reactivity cut; Ulysses cut as a companion; Strip split by loading screens; PS3 memory forced technical cuts.
- Fallout 4: underwater vault quest (20 Leagues Under the Sea), Danvers, "Off the Grid" main quest, Combat Zone arena stub, weapon models, Diamond City power-outage story.
- Fallout 76: no human NPCs at launch (added April 2020); Survival (Mar-Oct 2019), Nuclear Winter (June 2019 - late 2021) and Vault Raids (to Apr 2020) launched then removed.
- Pattern: most cuts are schedule/technical, not creative; the best what-ifs are systems (dual combat, rival party, post-ending play) rather than rooms.

## 2. Van Buren (Black Isle, cancelled Dec 8, 2003)

| Design element | Detail | Fate / reuse | Worth learning from? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dual-mode combat | Player chooses real-time or turn-based; the publisher reportedly mandated real-time and multiplayer | Never shipped; Fallout 3's V.A.T.S. is a different answer to the same problem | High: let one ruleset serve two play styles, but cost of dual balance is large |
| Prisoner protagonist | Starts in a quarantine prison cell; the player decides guilt or innocence | Not reused directly | High: a protagonist with a mystery and a moral choice up front |
| Race choice | Human, ghoul or super mutant | Not reused | Medium: strong replay value, huge reactivity cost |
| Main antagonist | Victor Presper, a frozen scientist, using the ODYSSEUS prison and orbital missile station B.O.M.B.-001 to cleanse the surface | Not reused | Medium |
| Prison robots chase | Escaped prisoners must be recovered to stop pursuit | Not reused | Medium: systemic pursuit as a consequence |
| Rival party | Fallout.wiki fetch did not describe a rival party; I could not verify it | [UNVERIFIED] | Unknown |
| Locations | Tibbets Prison (Leavenworth), Circle Junction, Denver, Boulder, Hoover Dam, PCTOWN, Fort Abandon, the Big Empty, Grand Canyon, Utah, Nevada, Colorado, Arizona | Hoover Dam and the Southwest setting reused in New Vegas | High: reusing a regional design doc is an efficient way to start |
| Factions | Brotherhood vs NCR war, Caesar's Legion, Daughters of Hecate, Caesar's Raiders, Jackals | Legion, Hoover Dam, caravan wars reused in NV; Daughters of Hecate not reused | High: shows how faction logic transfers |
| Companions | Companions make independent choices affecting the story | Echoes in NV companions | High |
| Player-chosen theme song for a skill | Unusual feedback flourish | Not reused | Low but charming |
| Railways for fast travel | Repair railway lines to unlock fast travel | Not reused | Medium: fast travel as earned infrastructure |
| Engine | "Jefferson" engine, NDL considered, tools struggled | Cancelled | Warning: new 3D tech with a team new to 3D was a major risk |

Why it was cancelled: Interplay's finances; Sawyer burned out and left; Black Isle closed. Why it matters: the 700-page design doc and tech demo leaked, so many ideas survive in public. Interplay's switch to Brotherhood of Steel shows a publisher choosing a cheaper console action game over an expensive RPG.

## 7. What the Cuts Tell Us

- **Cuts follow dependencies, not taste.** Voice storage, console memory, map size, loading zones, QA turnaround, publisher bonuses.
- **Cheap to author, expensive to cut late:** world size and voiced dialogue.
- **Systems what-ifs are the most valuable:** dual-mode combat, prisoner start, post-ending play, earned fast travel, independent companions.
- **Reuse is a legitimate strategy:** Boyarsky's cut Fallout 2 pitch became Old World Blues; Van Buren became New Vegas; Ulysses became Lonesome Road.

## Design Takeaways

- **Scope a post-ending epilogue from day one.** NV's cut shows players want to see consequences after the final choice, even small ones.
- **Show every major faction's home territory.** NV's Legion suffers because its core was cut; budget a full faction region or fewer factions.
- **Do not chop cities into zones.** FO3's D.C. shows how engine limits turn into complaints; plan settlement size around streaming.
- **Budget voice by disc/storage and production cost.** Cut dialogue late hurts more than writing less early.
- **Keep dual-mode combat only if one ruleset truly serves both.** Van Buren's ambition is attractive but costly; V.A.T.S. shows a lighter hybrid.
- **Keep a vivid, risky protagonist hook.** Prisoner-with-unknown-guilt is a stronger opening than a blank slate.
- **Never launch a world without people.** 76 needed Wastelanders; give players someone to talk to.
- **Playtest modes with the actual audience.** Survival and Nuclear Winter lived under two years; do small beta experiments.
- **Salvage cut characters and ideas.** Keep a bank of unused concepts; New Vegas and its DLC turned several into highlights.
